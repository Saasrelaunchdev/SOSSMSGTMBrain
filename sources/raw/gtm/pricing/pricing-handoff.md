**The offer in one line.** Contractors join free and never pay to see or decline a request; SOS SMS earns only on transactions, an SOS SMS fee on each customer request from the first one, and a contractor fee per completed paid job once the founding period ends.

**Status: decided 2026-10-03.** The revenue model (decision 2) and the trip charge (decision 5) are decided at the amounts below (`gtm/marketplace/resolution-register.md`), and copy may use them. Grade C figures stay internal. Nothing is live.

## Strategic rationale

**Who pays.** SOS SMS is a two-sided marketplace, and supply is the side that is hard to get: every customer promise depends on enough contractors in the zone to reach the density number. So contractors are subsidized, and the customer, who is paying for speed and the 90-minute guarantee, pays per request. Contractors start paying only after the founding period, and only when a job is completed and paid.

**Contractors: free to join, per completed job later.** $0 to join, $0 per request seen or declined, no subscription, no seat fee. After the founding period a contractor fee of $25 per completed paid job is deducted from the trip charge payout. Founding contractors keep $0 a job for 12 months after the founding period, written into the founding agreement from day one. The fee is built from the floor up and held far under the market: the cheapest per-lead option costs about $125 per booked job at a 20 percent close rate (S03, S06, grade C, internal only), so the fee leaves the contractor about $100 better off per booked job. It sits at the value ceiling (20 percent of that lead spend) and clears the cost floor ($10), leaving 86% of the fee as margin.

**Customers: free help, then two lines before dispatch.** Texting is free: the first reply gives stop-the-water steps, the safety line, who SOS SMS is, and the price, before any card. Dispatch costs two lines, never one number: the trip charge ($99, credited to the repair if the customer goes ahead and paid to the contractor) and the SOS SMS fee ($49). The fee clears its cost floor ($42, which carries the guarantee's refund reserve and card processing) leaving 70% of the fee as margin, and sits just under the value ceiling ($50, a quarter of the after-hours call fee a customer no longer pays separately, S50, grade C).

**Why the Hero columns are built this way.** For contractors the column to highlight is the founding offer: during recruiting every contractor who signs up is a founding contractor, and the offer is what makes signing up before launch worth it. For customers the Hero is Emergency dispatch; Help by text exists so value comes before payment.

**What the numbers say about growth.** Per completed job, SOS SMS nets about $31 in the founding period and about $56 after it. Paid search costs about $275 per paid job on the plumbing benchmark (S15, grade B, with a planned 50 percent text-to-paid rate), so a customer fee alone does not pay back paid search on the first job. At a 50 percent owned share and the contractor fee switched on, about 2.5 jobs pay back one paid customer. Owned share, repeat jobs, cheaper clicks, and the contractor fee are the levers.

## Notes for design

- **Two tables, two pages, never one.** The contractor table goes on the contractor page and the terms on one page; the customer table goes on the zone page and the guarantee page. No page shows both.
- **Highlight** the Founding contractor column and the Emergency dispatch column. Do not label either "Most popular": nothing is live.
- **No billing toggle.** There is no subscription on either side, so there is no monthly or annual switch and no "contact sales".
- **The trip charge and the SOS SMS fee are always two lines.** Never show a single total to a customer.
- **The safety line** stays on the customer table, and any page that invites an urgent text.
- **Contractor copy is future tense before launch** ("when SOS SMS opens in {{zone}}"). Customer pages stay held until the zone opens.
- **Merge fields.** {{SOS SMS number}} and {{zone}} are filled from the registered number and the zone record when a page is built; the covered areas and hours come from the same zone record. Nothing else on either table is left to fill.
- **Grade C figures stay internal.** Competitor prices and the after-hours call fee band live in the model, not on any page.
