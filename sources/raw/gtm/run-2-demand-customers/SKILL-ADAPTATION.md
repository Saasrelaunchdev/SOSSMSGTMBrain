# Running gtm-builder on SOS SMS, Run 2: Demand (Members)

The skill is `B2B SaaS Go-To-Market Builder`. This run sells the demand side of a two-sided marketplace for emergency home repair: a household membership, monthly or annual, joined before anything breaks, so that in an emergency a member texts one number and a vetted contractor commits to an arrival time. The buyer is a household, deciding over days, before the need. That changes the skill's ICP, buying committee, and channels, and it brings Phase 9's subscription logic back into play. Revised 2026-10-11, the membership revision.

Read this before Phase 1. Without it the run produces a firmographic ICP for a household, a three-person buying committee for one or two adults at a kitchen table, and a LinkedIn campaign aimed at homeowners.

## Phase 1. Product Feature Matrix

**Assumes:** one product with one buyer, its features scored on demand and competitiveness.

**Why it breaks:** SOS SMS has two buyers and two feature lists. Rating them together blends two demand curves into one quadrant chart that describes neither side.

**Instead:** rate only the items in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 3: nine from the source, two from the model (the membership itself, and the card and address on file that make the emergency one text), and four candidates. Run Steps 1.3 to 1.5 as written. Seed the source items with Bryan's ratings, labeled as hypotheses, and let the Step 1.1 research move them; score the model items from the member research directly. Rate the relaunch guarantee like any other item. Its handling is decided (resolution register R17): SOS SMS pays the second contractor's trip charge from membership revenue, and it stays out of copy until dispatch supports it. Steps 1.6 and 1.7 run unchanged.

**Result to watch for.** Plans paid in advance are common (S75 to S81, S93); a plan whose use is one text, with a money-backed 90-minute window from that text, is not. If Step 1.1 confirms it, the positioning leads with the one-text emergency and the guarantee, and the membership is the way in rather than the headline.

## Phase 2. Competitive Market Analysis

**Assumes:** the competitors are reviewed on G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot. Step 2.1 crawls them and Step 2.7 compares four and five star reviews against one to three star reviews.

**Why it breaks:** households do not review home plans on G2. They review plumbers on maps listings, and they complain about warranties and plans on consumer sites, the BBB, and forums.

**Instead:** keep Step 2.7's question, what satisfied members say they got and what unsatisfied ones say they did not, and ask it of these sources:

- The plan pages and terms of plumber clubs, warranties, and utility-partner plans: what is included, the waiting period, the caps, renewal and cancellation
- ConsumerAffairs, Trustpilot, and BBB complaints about warranties and plans: speed, denied claims, fees
- Maps and search reviews of the emergency contractors in the launch metro, for what an emergency is like without a plan
- Reddit homeowner and home repair communities: "is a home warranty worth it", "plumber membership worth it"

Slot 5, doing it yourself or waiting, is not a company. Profile it from the most viewed shutoff guides and the threads around them.

**Our own row.** Fill it from `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 1, not a crawl, and state strengths and weaknesses by design, with the weaknesses written plainly: no track record yet, open only in zones at density, and a fee every month or year before anything breaks. There is nothing live to review.

## Phase 4. ICP

**Assumes:** "ICP Level 1, MVP to PMF" and "ICP Level 2, Growth", with firmographic rows: revenue, team size, business model.

**Why it breaks:** the buyer is a household, not a company, and what defines them is a life event and a home, not a stage.

**Instead,** the B2C rewrite:

- **Level 1.** A new homeowner without a go-to plumber: new to the home or to the area.
- **Level 2.** An established homeowner, often in an older home, whose plumber does not answer at night or on weekends.

Replace the firmographic rows. "Revenue, $" becomes household income band, kept out as a targeting criterion (R39). "Team Size" becomes owner-occupier or small landlord. "Business Model" becomes what they already hold: nothing, a plumber's club, a warranty, a utility plan. Add rows for the trigger (moving in, the first freeze, a past emergency where nobody answered, a warranty claim that took days), home age and type, and where they can be reached before the need (a utility, an association, a newsletter, a guide).

**Result to watch for.** Before the need, a homeowner is reachable through people and places they already trust: their utility, their association, their newsletter, a partner who passes it on. That is cheaper than buying the moment of need, and it is the reason the model is a membership.

## Phase 5. Buying Committee

**A household.** One or two adults decide, over days, at a kitchen table or in an inbox. Fill the columns honestly: the person who reads the email or the partner's offer (user), the person who holds the card (manager), and the household's decision. Often one person is all three. Do not invent a procurement process.

The exception is a renter. The landlord is a second decision maker and the owner of the plumbing, which is why the demand side is homeowners (brief decision 1).

**Step 5.2** is the founding member area: the neighborhoods inside Run 1's founding contractor coverage, where memberships open once the density number is met (R55). Before then, the launch list (email, no charge, no service promised). Every Phase 8 asset targets inside it and nowhere else.

## Phase 6, the fabrication risk

Phase 6 asks for a "Supporting Proof Point" and a "Headline (Social Proof)" in each of nine copy drills. Eighteen fields asking for evidence.

SOS SMS has none. No members, no jobs, no arrival times on record. In every drill the social proof headline is a method line and the proof point names the record that will show the claim is true. Method and process are legitimate substitutes for the social proof headline: how it works in one text, what membership includes, what the trip charge buys, and what happens when the 90-minute window is missed.

**Four banned forms this side reaches for:** an average arrival time, a count of homes or members, a savings figure ("pays for itself"), and repair coverage ("like a warranty"). None exists.

**The safety line.** Any drill that invites an urgent text carries the 911 line from `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. It is not optional copy.

**Cross-side drift.** Check every headline against `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 6 before closing the phase.

## Phase 7. Customer Journey Funnel

**Runs as written,** across two clocks: weeks before the need (stages 1 to 4, awareness to joining) and minutes during it (stage 5, the emergency). Stage 7, "Renewal and Loyalty", now means what the skill assumes: a recurring membership that renews or does not. Measure monthly churn and annual renewal, plus referrals.

**Add these to Primary Journey Blockers.** Each is an infrastructure problem that will look like a funnel problem:

- **Another subscription.** A fee every month or year before anything breaks. The answer is what membership includes, the trip charge only on use, and cancel any time, stated before the card.
- **Is my home covered at night.** Memberships open per zone at the density number, for its covered hours. The membership page names both, and the ZIP check says plainly when a home is outside.
- **Can the text get through.** Business texting needs the sending number registered with carriers (10DLC). Unregistered traffic gets filtered, and the member sees silence.
- **What if no one accepts.** What the member is told, and when. Decision 3 answers it: told within 15 minutes, nothing charged.
- **A card that fails.** The card on file is billed monthly or annually; a failed card is caught at billing, before an emergency finds it.

## Phase 8. Assets and Collaterals

**Assumes three campaigns, hardcoded:** LinkedIn Outreach via HeyReach, Cold Email via Instantly, and LinkedIn Ads.

**Why it breaks:** households are not prospected on LinkedIn, and SOS SMS has no sales team (brief, Classification).

**Instead,** keep the three-channel by three-funnel-stage grid with the pre-need channels the membership makes possible (membership revision, 2026-10-11):

1. **Affiliates, before the need.** Partners who already reach homeowners pass SOS SMS on, with a code and a disclosed commission per new paid member (R61): utilities and cities (the partner-led model, S93), homeowner and neighborhood associations, local publications and newsletters, home content creators, home improvement retailers, insurance agents for existing policyholders, employers' benefit platforms. Real estate agents, lenders, title and settlement agents, and home inspectors may share it with no payment (RESPA, S86, S94). Every affiliate discloses that they are paid (S88).
2. **Email, before the need.** SOS SMS's own homeowner list, built from the readiness scorecard and the launch list; partner sends; member lifecycle email (welcome, seasonal notes, renewal). CAN-SPAM throughout (S73).
3. **Search and SEO pages, for sign-ups.** Prevention and problem guides read before the need, the membership page, zone pages; paid search (Google, then Microsoft) and paid social for sign-ups, geofenced to open zones, with no retargeting and no pixels. Never ads for dispatch during an emergency, and never Local Services Ads (S70).

Plus the **member loops**: the saved contact card, Text READY, the forwardable card, the after-job two questions, renewal.

**The constraint is supply.** A membership sold where too few contractors are available produces refunds under the 90-minute guarantee and cancellations. Memberships open only in zones at the density number (R55), and sign-ups pause when acceptance drops (R34).

**SMS as a marketing channel.** Marketing texts need separate written consent (S31). Transactional texts stay transactional.

## Phase 9. Pricing and Packaging

**Assumes a subscription,** with value metrics per user, seat, usage, outcome, or capability, and four plans arranged as Decoy, Hero, and Anchor.

**Fits, narrowed:** the value metric is per household, and there is one membership, billed monthly or annually. Four plans would sell a choice the member does not need to make; the annual option is the only second shape.

1. The membership: planning values $10 a month or $100 a year (R51, R52), set against the market band (plumber clubs $8.25 to $19.99, S75 to S77; utility-partner plans $16.99 to $22.99, S78; warranties $47 to $82, S79) and a cost floor; copy uses `{{monthly price}}` and `{{annual price}}` until the launch price is set in Stage 0.
2. The trip charge on use: $99, set by SOS SMS per trade and zone, credited to the repair (decision 5), charged to the card on file at acceptance.
3. The 90-minute refund as a cost line: planned at 5 percent of requests for misses SOS SMS causes, funded from membership revenue; contractor-caused misses are funded from the trip charge (decision 3, Section 6 row 1).
4. The unit economics per member: contribution a month, monthly churn, annual renewal, and the acquisition ceiling per paid member by channel (R58 to R63).
5. The subsidy question, shared with Run 1: members fund the network; contractors join free and pay $25 per completed paid job after the founding period.

**Step 9.3, ROI anchoring, still runs.** Anchor 2, the alternative comparison, is what a household pays for the same readiness elsewhere (the market band) and what it pays without any plan when something breaks (an after-hours call fee, S50, C). No savings figure is written.

## Lead Magnet Scorecard

Runs as written, and matters more now. "How Ready Is Your Home for a 2 a.m. Leak?" is the top of the email list: do you know where your water shutoff is, do you have someone who comes at night. In an open zone it ends with joining; in a zone not yet open, with the launch list; outside, with the plan alone. Its outputs are scores and steps, never damage-cost figures without a source.

## 10-Slide Deck

Runs as written, once per run. A combined marketplace deck is a separate step after both runs. Its own prompt's rule on unknown metrics applies with extra force: no invented number, and every SOS SMS figure shown as decided, a planning value, or illustrative.
