# Phase 6. Value Proposition Dashboard, customer side

Three pillars, nine reasons to believe, nine copy drills. Written to `../GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 and checked against Section 6.

**The fabrication rule for this phase.** SOS SMS has no customers, jobs, or arrival times on record. Every "Headline (Social Proof)" and every "Supporting Proof Point" is {TBD}, with a method substitute where useful. No drill states or implies an average arrival time, a count of homes helped, or that SOS SMS prevents damage.

**Brackets** mark scope that open decisions must settle. A drill with an open bracket does not go out.

**Safety line.** Every drill whose call to action invites a text carries: "For fire, a gas smell, or anyone hurt, call 911 first." Gas utilities tell people to leave the building before calling and not to use phones near a leak (S60), so for gas the line becomes: "Smell gas? Get out first, then call 911 and your gas company."

## Step 6.1. Brand promise and positioning

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Brand Promise | Text once, and a vetted contractor is on the way, with the trip charge stated before anyone comes |
| Positioning | The emergency repair line you text instead of calling down a list. Built around two things: one message that reaches the vetted contractors for the trade at once, and an arrival window that costs SOS SMS money when it is missed |

## Proof capture plan

1. The terms of service carry the permission clause before the first customer (Phase 5).
2. After every completed job, one text: "Did your contractor arrive within the window? Would you tell a neighbor?" Answers recorded verbatim with the date.
3. Arrival times are logged from acceptance to arrival, so that a real figure can one day replace {TBD}, with its sample size and date, and only if it holds up.
