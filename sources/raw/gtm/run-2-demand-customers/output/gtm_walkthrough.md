# SLIDE 1, SOS SMS Customer GTM: What the Dashboard Suite Is For

- SOS SMS is a pre-launch, two-sided marketplace for emergency home repair, and this deck covers the customer side
- Nine dashboards plus a lead magnet answer one question: why would a homeowner text SOS SMS instead of calling down a list?
- The answer the suite builds toward: one text reaches the vetted contractors nearby, one accepts with an arrival time, and a missed 90-minute window costs SOS SMS money
- Every SOS SMS metric is a planning value until the zone runs, and no arrival time is claimed
- So what: this side opens only in zones where the contractor side has reached density

---

# SLIDE 2, Product Feature Matrix Dashboard: What We Learned and What We Lead With

- Hero features: the 90-minute guarantee, one text that reaches the network, the arrival time on acceptance, and privacy in its narrow form
- The guarantee moved up: only one competitor in the set has a money-backed arrival promise, and it covers scheduled appointments
- Text-only and privacy are not unique, so they lead as mechanics, not as "only we"
- The research's strongest new finding: a price range before arrival is wanted by all and offered by few; it is not offered at launch (R49)
- So what: lead with the guarantee and one reply; the trip charge, stated before dispatch, stands in for a price range at launch

---

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis Dashboard: Competitor Map and Buying Context

- Six alternatives: searching and calling, Roto-Rooter, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Angi, Thumbtack, and the home warranty line
- Customers praise availability, punctuality guarantees, and money-back guarantees on the work
- They complain about no-shows, unanswered calls at night, surprise after-hours fees, and floods of calls from quote sites
- SOS SMS checks licenses and insurance, not workmanship, and says so
- So what: compete on arrival and on one reply, not on brand or on guarantees for the repair

---

# SLIDE 4, Positioning Dashboard: Four Vectors, Current State and Projected State

- They lead: always-open brand (Roto-Rooter) and guarantees on the work (Angi, Thumbtack)
- We lead, projected: guaranteed arrival window (8.8 of 10) and many contractors reached with one reply (9.0 of 10)
- Current state: 0 on every vector, because nothing is live
- Positioning line: the one text that reaches the vetted contractors nearby, with money back if they miss the window
- So what: the guarantee is only honest where density is met, which makes density the moat as well as the constraint

---

# SLIDE 5, ICP Dashboard: Who We Win First, and Why

- Homeowners inside the founding zone, in two groups: no go-to contractor, and a contractor who does not answer at night
- Reach both in the moment, where they search for help: bottom-of-funnel search ads and problem pages
- Triggers: the emergency itself, moving in, the first freeze, a neighbor's story
- Renters and property managers are outside this run
- So what: paid search buys the first request from a household; the saved number has to bring the next one

---

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee Dashboard: Personas, Blockers, Drivers

- The committee collapses: the person at home with the problem decides, in minutes
- Blockers: a payment link from an unknown number looks like a scam, no card at hand, fear of being overcharged
- Drivers: someone coming with a time, the cost before anyone comes, not being flooded with calls
- The founding customer area is the neighborhoods inside the founding contractor zone
- So what: the first reply text has to earn trust before it asks for anything

---

# SLIDE 7, Customer Journey Funnel Dashboard: Stage Progression and Internal KPIs

- Product-led entry: a search ad or problem page, a text, and stop-the-water help before any payment
- Evaluation happens in the first reply: who we are, the trip charge, the guarantee scope, then the link
- KPIs: time to acceptance, share arriving within the window, refund rate, saved-number rate
- Blockers: paying by text at night, link trust, carrier registration, and the no-acceptance case
- So what: refunds paid automatically are what make the guarantee believable

---

# SLIDE 8, Lead Magnet: How Ready Is Your Home for a 2 a.m. Leak?

- Seven questions on the shutoff, the water heater, the home's age, and who to call at night
- Immediate value: a readiness score, a three-step plan, and a shutoff guide
- Every tier ends with Text READY, which saves the SOS SMS number in the customer's phone
- No damage-cost figures without a source, and the 911 line on the results page
- So what: the product is the lead magnet, so the first emergency text is the second contact

# SLIDE 9, Assets and Collaterals Library Dashboard: Channel Coverage and TOFU to BOFU Flow

- Product-led, no outreach: bottom-of-funnel paid search (Google, then Microsoft), problem-solving pages, and product loops
- Problem ad groups and guides lead with stop-the-water steps; emergency ad groups lead with text-from-ad
- Bottom of funnel: the first reply and the guarantee in full
- Ads run only in zones and hours at density, and never present SOS SMS as the plumber; Local Services Ads are closed to a dispatch model
- So what: every asset targets the founding zone and nowhere else

---

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging Dashboard: Value Metric, Usage Metric, Tiers, Expansion Levers

- Value metric: per request, a trip charge credited to the repair plus an SOS SMS fee, both shown before dispatch
- Usage metric: requests per 1,000 homes in the zone per month
- Packaging: one standard request with the guarantee: a $99 trip charge and a $49 SOS SMS fee; no membership at launch
- Paid search runs about $137 to $216 per first text on plumbing benchmarks, so a customer fee alone will not pay back the first job
- Expansion levers: more trades behind the same number, neighbor referral, and new zones at density
- So what: two lines before dispatch, $99 and $49, against the after-hours service call fee customers already know
