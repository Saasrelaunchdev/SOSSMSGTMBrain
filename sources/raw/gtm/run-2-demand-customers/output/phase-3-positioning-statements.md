## Step 3.1. Positioning statements

**Against 24/7 plumbing brands and on-demand apps (Roto-Rooter, Benjamin Franklin, InstaService):**
Not "another plumbing company", one text that reaches the licensed, insured contractors on SOS SMS for your trade, all at once. Whoever can come accepts with an arrival time, and if they are not there within 90 minutes of your request, you get the $99 trip charge back.

- Must be true before use: the zone meets the density number (Run 1 Phase 5); the guarantee scope is stated as decided (decision 3); the covered areas and hours are named in the same place.
- Benjamin Franklin pays $5 a minute for lateness on scheduled appointments (S40). The difference to state, when asked: SOS SMS's window applies to an emergency text, not to a booked appointment.

**Against quote marketplaces and the home warranty line (Angi, Thumbtack, American Home Shield):**
Not "a quote site" or "a claim line", a way to get someone to your door tonight without handing your number to a crowd or waiting for a claim to be assigned. Only the contractor who takes your job gets your address and details.

- Must be true before use: the data rule (brief decision 6) is in the privacy policy, in the same words.
- For a covered failure under a warranty plan, the warranty is usually the cheaper route. Say so if asked; it is what makes the rest of the statement believable.
- AHS's contract allows 48 hours on weekends to contact a contractor and does not guarantee timely emergency service (S20). Internal comparison; cite only after verifying the current contract.

**Against the status quo (searching and calling down the list, or waiting until morning):**
Not "one more number to try", the one text you send instead of calling down a list at 2 a.m. You see the trip charge before anyone is sent, and it counts toward the work if you go ahead.

- Must be true before use: the trip charge is shown and agreed by text before dispatch.
- Waiting has a cost: EPA guidance is to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold (S58). Use as context, never as a claim that SOS SMS prevents damage.

## Positioning in one line

For homeowners with a plumbing or water emergency and no one answering, SOS SMS is the one text that reaches the vetted contractors nearby, with an arrival time when one accepts and money back if they miss the window.

## What this positioning deliberately does not claim

| Do not claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "Every contractor in your area" | Only contractors on SOS SMS are reached (brief Section 5) |
| Any average or typical arrival time | None exists; pre-launch |
| That SOS SMS prevents damage or saves money | No evidence; the guarantee covers arrival, not outcome |
| A guarantee on the repair itself | Not offered (decided, R21); SOS SMS checks licenses and insurance, not workmanship |
| "Emergency service" or anything that reads as 911 | The name invites it; the safety line is required instead |
| "Never shared" | The accepting contractor must receive the address and details |
