# Speed-to-scale plan

How SOS SMS grows once a zone works: product-led, with no sales team, and with speed chosen over efficiency where the market rewards it. This plan applies a set of scaling principles to SOS SMS's two-sided, local marketplace. It sits on top of both runs and changes no claim in them.

The principles, in one paragraph: in a market where the advantage goes to whoever reaches scale first rather than whoever launches first, a company may deliberately accept inefficiency, in spending, in process, in polish, to grow faster than the competition. That bet only makes sense when the business model can pay off at scale, and it has to be managed in stages, with guardrails, and with a clear sense of when to stop.

Every SOS SMS number below is a decided value, a planning value with its basis, or labeled illustrative, from `resolution-register.md`; none is a result. Market figures carry source IDs from `../research/SOURCES.md`.

**Revised 2026-10-11, the membership revision.** The demand side is a monthly or annual membership, joined before the need, so the engine of each zone is now partner-led and owned acquisition before anything breaks (affiliates, email, the launch list), with paid social for sign-ups only where it comes in under the acquisition ceiling. Sections 1 to 5, 7, 8, 9, and 10 carry the revised economics; the contractor side is unchanged.

**Revised 2026-10-20, the tiers revision.** One membership covers plumbing, HVAC, and electrical, and members choose Basic, Plus, or Premier. A zone opens only when all three trades meet the density number in every hour band. No paid search at launch: affiliates, email, the launch list, and paid social sell memberships before the need. A late arrival keeps its trip charge; the 90-minute guarantee on Plus and Premier is a credit of 6 months of membership on Plus and 12 on Premier (12 since 2026-10-21). Sections 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10 carry the revised words.

## 1. Does speed decide the winner in this market?

Speed is worth paying for when a market is large, has network effects, and is being contested now. SOS SMS meets the first two conditions in a specific way:

| Condition | SOS SMS | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Large market | Yes. 85.7 million owner-occupied homes (S62, A); water damage and freezing claims at about 1 in 60 insured homes a year (S57, B); about 504,500 plumbing jobs on the supply side (S23, A); heating or AC repair is the most common home emergency, 30 percent of homeowners in a year (S82, B) | Room for a large business: three trades from the first zone, and more trades later |
| Network effects | Yes, but **local**. More contractors in a zone means faster acceptance, which wins more members there, which keeps contractors busy there. A contractor in one metro does nothing for a member in another | The race is run zone by zone and metro by metro, not nationally |
| Lock-in | Weak on the supply side: contractors also use Angi, Thumbtack, and Google (S03 to S08). Stronger on the member side: a membership renews and the number is already saved, and monthly churn measures it (R59) | Speed buys density. Loyalty has to come from the product: the saved number, the trip paid before rolling, the guarantee on Plus and Premier, and a membership worth keeping in the months nothing breaks |
| Contested now | Partly. Plumber clubs, warranties, and utility-partner plans are established, and HomeServe alone has more than 1,300 partners (S93); no one in the researched set offers SOS SMS's combination (`../research/`) | There is a window to reach density, and members, first in chosen zones |

**Conclusion.** Grow fast **inside** a zone once it is proven, and fast **across** zones only once the zone playbook repeats with predictable economics. Before a zone is proven, grow carefully and learn.

## 3. The two growth constraints

### Product and market fit, proven per zone

Not proven anywhere yet. The signals below decide whether a zone has it. The thresholds are set **before** launch (resolution register, R32), so the result cannot be argued after the fact.

| Signal | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance | Share of requests accepted inside the remaining window, in each trade | 90 percent or more |
| Arrival | Share of accepted jobs arriving inside the window | 95 percent or more |
| Late arrivals and no-shows | Per 100 accepted requests | 5 or fewer |
| Monthly churn | Share of monthly members who cancel in a month | 6.5 percent or less (R59) |
| Cost per new paid member | Acquisition spend, affiliate commissions included, ÷ new paid members, by channel | At or under the ceiling of the plan joined: about $47 Basic, $70 Plus, $151 Premier (R63) |
| Contractor activity | Share of contractors accepting at least one job in the last 30 days, in plumbing and HVAC; over 6 months in electrical, where requests are rarer (R64) | 60 percent or more |

### Operational scalability, without a sales or operations army

A product-led company with no sales team scales only what the product does on its own. Everything a person does in the first zone has to become software, or a repeatable kit, before the zone after next.

| Operation | First zone | Before scaling across zones |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor verification | Founders check each trade's license and insurance by hand | License and insurance checks in onboarding; recheck reminders |
| Dispatch | Software, with founders watching and stepping in | Software only; alerts when acceptance falls |
| Payment hold, release, payout | Payment provider, founders reconcile | Automated; exceptions queue |
| Late-arrival credits, Plus and Premier | Applied automatically to the next bill from day one | Same |
| Disputes | Founders handle | Rules plus a small queue |
| Safety triage | Automated reply, founders review | Automated, audited |
| Membership billing, renewals, cancellations | Payment provider; founders handle exceptions | Automated, with renewal reminders and an exceptions queue |
| Affiliates | Signed by the demand lead; codes issued by hand | The affiliate program page, the kit, and generated codes and statements |
| Sign-ups and the launch list | Built by hand for one zone | A zone template: the launch list sequence, the opening email, affiliate codes, paid social sign-up audiences, the membership page |
| Contractor recruiting | Cold email and one supply house per trade | A zone recruiting kit for each trade: list build, sequences, partner pack |

## 4. Speed over efficiency: where to spend to go faster, and where never to

| Accept inefficiency here | Why it is worth it |
|---|---|
| Affiliate commissions and sign-up spend up to the ceiling in proving zones | They buy members fast, and with them the answers that matter: acceptance by hour and trade, late arrivals, churn, and the plan mix. Waiting for word of mouth alone would take seasons |
| Free entry for contractors: $0 to join, $0 per request | Supply is the constrained side; every contractor added raises acceptance for every member in the zone, and each pays only from completed paid jobs |
| Recruiting more contractors than the density model asks for | Buffers nights, weekends, freeze spikes, and heat waves, when everyone in a trade is busy at once |
| Applying the late-arrival credit generously and automatically | Each credit is the cost of a believable promise on Plus and Premier |
| A plain, text-only product | Nothing to download is also nothing to build. Polish comes later |
| Founders covering dispatch gaps by hand in the first zone | It keeps promises while the software learns |

| Never trade for speed | Why |
|---|---|
| Accuracy of claims | No invented proof, arrival times, or counts (both briefs, Section 5). A growth story built on a made-up number fails at scale |
| Safety triage before dispatch | Gas, fire, sparks, a burning smell, a shock, or injury go to 911 and the utility first (S60) |
| Paying contractors on time | It is the reason they joined |
| Crediting members automatically | It is the reason they trust the guarantee |
| Easy cancellation | Cancel any time, on the membership page or by texting HELP (R56); a membership that is hard to leave breaks the law and the trust (S87) |
| Privacy as promised | One breach of "only the contractor who accepts sees your address" undoes VP2 |
| Messaging, email, subscription, and affiliate law, and ad policy | Marketing texts need written consent (S31); all email follows CAN-SPAM (S73); automatic renewal terms and consent (S87); affiliates disclose payment (S88); no payments at a home purchase settlement (S86, S94); Google's rules on local services, should a brand campaign ever run (S70, S71) |

## 5. Unit economics at speed

The engine on the member side is the membership: a member pays every month or year, before anything breaks, and the contribution left after costs is what acquisition is paid from. Illustrative, from the pricing model at the decided prices and the planning values (R51 to R70):

| Measure, monthly plan | Basic, $10 | Plus, $15 | Premier, $35 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution a month, with the $25 contractor fee from the first job | about $9.29 | about $13.77 | about $29.62 |
| First 6 months of contribution: the acquisition ceiling (R63) | about $47 | about $70 | about $151 |
| Lifetime contribution at 6.5 percent churn (R59) | about $143 | about $212 | about $456 |
| Plan mix target (R70) | 10 percent | 80 percent | 10 percent |

| Measure, annual plan | Basic, $100 | Plus, $150 | Premier, $350 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution a year | about $95.31 | about $139.78 | about $278.05 |
| First 6 months, paid up front | about $48 | about $70 | about $139 |
| Lifetime contribution at 75 percent renewal (R60) | about $381 | about $559 | about $1,112 |

At the plan mix a member contributes about $14.91 a month, and one zone of about 410 members about $6,134 a month on monthly billing.

Dispatch starts 30 days after joining on a monthly plan (R54), and at once on an annual plan.

**Acquisition cost per new paid member = channel cost ÷ new paid members from it**, by channel and zone, with affiliate commissions counted in it.

| Channel | Cost per new paid member | Against the ceilings (about $47, $70, $151) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliates | $20, the commission (R61), paid once the member's second monthly payment or first annual payment clears | Under every plan's ceiling, by about $27 on Basic; paid back in the third month on Basic, the second on Plus | Market range $4 to $56 an enrollment (S85, C) |
| Email, SOS SMS's own list | Counted from the first opening email | Measured | Open and click benchmarks only (S92, C) |
| Paid social, sign-ups before the need | Counted in each open zone | Scales only at or under the plan's ceiling; pauses with sign-ups (R34) | Measured |
| Paid search, plumbing benchmark | about $137: $10.49 a click, 7.63 percent converting, assumed equal for sign-ups | Over, about 3 times Basic's; not run at launch (decision 11) | S15, B |

**Paid search at plumbing benchmarks will not pay back a new member inside 6 months, so there is none at launch (decision 11).** Affiliates and the launch list do pay back, which is why they lead and paid social supports. Search volume for SOS SMS by name is counted once a zone has been open 6 months (R68). Every channel scales while it is at or under the ceiling of the plan joined and stops when it is not (decision 12). Five levers make the model work:

| Lever | Effect | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Partner-led acquisition | Affiliates and email bring members in under the ceiling, before the need | Cost per new paid member = commission or send cost ÷ joins |
| 2. Retention | Every point of monthly churn removed raises what a member is worth | First 6 months = contribution a month × (1 - (1 - churn)^6) ÷ churn; lifetime = contribution a month ÷ churn |
| 3. Annual plans and three trades | The membership page shows Plus on the annual plan first; annual members pay up front and renew at about 75 percent (R60), an annual Plus member worth about $559 against about $212 on the monthly plan; three trades in one membership give a member more reasons to stay | Contribution a year ÷ (1 - renewal) |
| 4. The plan ladder | Plus keeps about $4.49 a month more than Basic for a 6-month credit that costs about $0.17 and 5 phones that cost $0.20 more; Premier about $20.33 more. Plus is built for 80 percent of members (R70) | Contribution by plan (pricing model) |
| 5. Revenue from both sides | $25 per completed paid job from contractors, from the first job, adds about $0.89 a Basic or Plus member a month at 0.50 requests a member a year (R58), 90 percent accepted and 95 percent arriving, and makes every request pay for itself, about $13.03 net | $25 × 0.50 × 0.90 × 0.95 ÷ 12 |

**Decided (decision 2, revised 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21):** members choose Basic ($10 a month or $100 a year), Plus ($15 or $150, the 6-month credit and up to 5 phones), or Premier ($35 or $350, the 12-month credit, a trip charge a year, a second home, and up to 10 phones) (R51, R52, R65, R66, R69), with no fee per request; contractors pay $0 to join and per request and a $25 per-completed-job contractor fee from their first job, far below the Run 1 ceiling and written into the contractor agreement, the same for every contractor. Treat affiliates and the launch list as the engine that opens each zone, and paid social as support that runs only under the ceiling. The live model is `google-folder/SOS_SMS_Pricing_Model.xlsx`: every input on its first sheet, Founder Inputs.

**Funding implication.** Members join before the need, but a zone cannot open until supply reaches density, so each zone's first months go to recruiting contractors in all three trades, 27 places (R08), and growing its launch list before any membership revenue arrives. Runway of at least 18 months at Stage A spending (R35) has to cover several zones' proving periods at once. Raise for the plan's spending, with margin for it taking longer than hoped.

## 6. Stages of scale, and what changes at each

Organizations change shape as they grow, and what works at one size breaks at the next. Stages here are by headcount.

| Stage | Team | Zones | What the founders do | Hires that make the next stage possible | How decisions get made |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Under 10 people | One zone, three trades | Everything: product, affiliates, email, contractor support, dispatch backup. Both founders work supply first in Stage 1, while the demand lead signs affiliates and grows the launch list; at Stage 2 one becomes the supply lead and the other the demand lead (R25) | A partnerships and lifecycle email operator; a product engineer who owns dispatch, payments, and membership billing | Founders decide everything, daily |
| B | Tens | Several zones, then a second metro | Design the zone template and recruiting kit; stop doing each zone by hand | Supply growth (email and partners); member partnerships at metro scale (utilities, cities, associations); trust, safety, payments, and billing operations; analytics | Small teams own a metric each; weekly review against the zone signals |
| C | Hundreds | Many metros | Allocate capital across metros; hire leaders | Metro launch teams; compliance; regional operations | Leaders own metros; the founders own the plan |
| D and E | Thousands and beyond | National, many trades | Out of scope for this plan | | |

Rules that go with the stages:

- **Hire for the next stage, not forever.** The right partnerships operator for one zone is not necessarily the right head of growth for twenty.
- **Accept imperfect management early.** Process that would be overhead at Stage A becomes necessary at Stage B. Add it then, not before.
- **Change the playbook when the stage changes.** What worked in the first zone (founders stepping in) becomes a liability in the tenth.

## 7. The counterintuitive rules, applied to SOS SMS

| Rule | Applied |
|---|---|
| Ship before it is polished | Launch text-only, no app, minimal website, one zone. But not before the four things that must work on day one: the plan terms and the guarantee, safety triage, carrier registration (S32), and payouts |
| Choose which problems to leave unfixed | Leave: website polish, a fourth trade, non-emergency scheduling, broad content. Never leave: credits and no-show refunds, payouts, safety, claim accuracy, privacy, easy cancellation |
| Do things that do not scale, in the first zone only | Founders reconcile payouts and cover dispatch gaps by hand. Each manual task gets a date by which software replaces it |
| Listen to members selectively | Build what shortens the path from the request text to arrival, and what keeps the membership worth keeping. Defer requests outside it, however reasonable (scheduled maintenance, quotes for remodels) |
| Raise more than the plan needs | Supply is recruited and the launch list grown before any membership revenue arrives (section 5) |
| Keep the culture deliberate as it grows | Write down the non-negotiables in section 4 now, so new hires inherit them |

## 8. When to accelerate, and when to slow down

| Gate | Condition to pass | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Zone proven | Every section 3 signal at its threshold for 4 consecutive weeks | Accelerate in the zone: affiliate and paid social spend raised while cost per new paid member holds under the ceiling and acceptance holds in every trade and hour band |
| 2. Zone full | Every trade and hour band holding the density number; members growing toward about 410 (R64) | Open the adjacent zone with the template |
| 3. Metro proven | Three zones pass Gate 1 with the template, without founders stepping in | Open a second metro with a metro launch team |
| 4. Trade added | Plumbing, HVAC, and electrical density hold; a referred trade (water damage, then locksmith) reaches its own density | A fourth trade behind the same membership |

**Slow down or stop** in a zone when late arrivals, acceptance, monthly churn, or contractor activity cross their thresholds, or when a channel's cost per new paid member rises above the ceiling of the plan joined (decision 12, R63). Speed is a choice made zone by zone, not a commitment that cannot be reversed.

## 9. Guardrails for scaling responsibly

- Nothing invented, ever. Pre-launch figures are planning values with their basis, never results; proof needs permission (both briefs).
- Safety triage before dispatch (cross-side check, item F).
- Contractors' terms in writing, before sign-up; payouts on time.
- Privacy as promised; no ad retargeting.
- Compliance: TCPA consent for marketing texts (S31); CAN-SPAM for all email (S73); ROSCA and state automatic renewal laws for the membership (S87); FTC Endorsement Guides for affiliates (S88); RESPA, no payments at a home purchase settlement (S86, S94); Google ad policies (S70, S71), should a brand campaign ever run, including never presenting SOS SMS as the contractor.
- Fair treatment of contractors in the late-arrival rule: a late arrival keeps its trip charge; two late arrivals or no-shows in 90 days pause requests until a person reviews them (R67).

## 10. Risks of scaling this fast

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition costs more than a member is worth | A channel's cost per new paid member above the ceiling (R63) | Pause that channel; shift spend to affiliates and the launch list; push Plus on the annual plan |
| Members leave in quiet months | Monthly churn above 6.5 percent (R59) | The readiness kit, seasonal notes, annual plans, and renewal reminders that say plainly what renews; never make cancelling hard (S87) |
| Dependence on one partner or one ad platform | A partner ends its program, or a social ad policy changes | Several affiliates per zone, SOS SMS's own email list, and organic pages live from the start |
| Too few requests for contractors | Members per zone far below about 410 (R64); contractor activity falls, first in electrical, where requests are rarest | Grow the launch list and affiliates before a zone opens; recruit to the density number, not past it; tell each contractor the expected volume for their trade before they join |
| Thin zones break the guarantee | Late arrivals rise at night, in freezes, or in heat waves | Sign-ups pause in the zone (R34); recruit for the thin trade and hour bands before reopening sign-ups; requests keep going out and the guarantee keeps holding (R15) |
| Incumbents respond | Plumber clubs, warranties, or quote marketplaces add an arrival guarantee | Density is the defense: a guarantee needs contractors on the ground |
| Contractors multi-home and get poached | Contractor activity falls | Payout speed, the trip charge kept on a late arrival, and no charge for any request they do not win |
