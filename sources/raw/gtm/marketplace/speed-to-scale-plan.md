# Speed-to-scale plan

How SOS SMS grows once a zone works: product-led, with no sales team, and with speed chosen over efficiency where the market rewards it. This plan applies a set of scaling principles to SOS SMS's two-sided, local marketplace. It sits on top of both runs and changes no claim in them.

The principles, in one paragraph: in a market where the advantage goes to whoever reaches scale first rather than whoever launches first, a company may deliberately accept inefficiency, in spending, in process, in polish, to grow faster than the competition. That bet only makes sense when the business model can pay off at scale, and it has to be managed in stages, with guardrails, and with a clear sense of when to stop.

Every SOS SMS number below is a decided value, a planning value with its basis, or labeled illustrative, from `resolution-register.md`; none is a result. Market figures carry source IDs from `../research/SOURCES.md`.

---

## 1. Does speed decide the winner in this market?

Speed is worth paying for when a market is large, has network effects, and is being contested now. SOS SMS meets the first two conditions in a specific way:

| Condition | SOS SMS | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Large market | Yes. 85.7 million owner-occupied homes (S62, A); water damage and freezing claims at about 1 in 60 insured homes a year (S57, B); about 504,500 plumbing jobs on the supply side (S23, A) | Room for a large business, once emergency trades beyond plumbing are added |
| Network effects | Yes, but **local**. More contractors in a zone means faster acceptance, which wins more customers there, which keeps contractors busy there. A contractor in one metro does nothing for a customer in another | The race is run zone by zone and metro by metro, not nationally |
| Lock-in | Weak. Contractors also use Angi, Thumbtack, and Google (S03 to S08); customers search fresh each time | Speed buys density, not loyalty. Loyalty has to come from the product: the saved number, the trip paid before rolling, the guarantee |
| Contested now | Partly. Quote marketplaces and national brands are established; no one in the researched set offers SOS SMS's combination (`../research/`) | There is a window to reach density first in chosen zones |

**Conclusion.** Grow fast **inside** a zone once it is proven, and fast **across** zones only once the zone playbook repeats with predictable economics. Before a zone is proven, grow carefully and learn.

## 3. The two growth constraints

### Product and market fit, proven per zone

Not proven anywhere yet. The signals below decide whether a zone has it. The thresholds are set **before** launch (resolution register, R32), so the result cannot be argued after the fact.

| Signal | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance | Share of requests accepted inside the remaining window | 90 percent or more |
| Arrival | Share of accepted jobs arriving inside the window | 95 percent or more |
| SOS-caused refunds | Refunds SOS SMS pays, per 100 requests | 5 or fewer |
| Paid conversion | Share of first texts that become paid requests | 50 percent or more |
| Owned share | Share of requests that came from a saved number, a share, or an organic page rather than an ad | 30 percent or more by the end of month 6; 50 percent by month 12 |
| Contractor activity | Share of contractors accepting at least one job in the last 30 days | 60 percent or more |

### Operational scalability, without a sales or operations army

A product-led company with no sales team scales only what the product does on its own. Everything a person does in the first zone has to become software, or a repeatable kit, before the zone after next.

| Operation | First zone | Before scaling across zones |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor verification | Founders check licenses and insurance by hand | License and insurance checks in onboarding; recheck reminders |
| Dispatch | Software, with founders watching and stepping in | Software only; alerts when acceptance falls |
| Payment hold, release, payout | Payment provider, founders reconcile | Automated; exceptions queue |
| Guarantee refunds | Paid automatically from day one | Same |
| Disputes | Founders handle | Rules plus a small queue |
| Safety triage | Automated reply, founders review | Automated, audited |
| Ad campaigns | Built by hand for one zone | A zone template: keywords, ads, geofence, schedule, landing page |
| Contractor recruiting | Cold email and one supply house | A zone recruiting kit: list build, sequences, partner pack |

## 4. Speed over efficiency: where to spend to go faster, and where never to

| Accept inefficiency here | Why it is worth it |
|---|---|
| Paid search spending above break-even in proving zones | It buys answers fast: acceptance rates by hour, paid conversion, refund rates. Waiting for organic demand would take seasons |
| $0 to contractors during the founding period | Supply is the constrained side; every contractor added raises acceptance for every customer in the zone |
| Recruiting more contractors than the density model asks for | Buffers nights, weekends, and freeze spikes, when everyone is busy at once |
| Paying guarantee refunds generously and automatically | Each refund is the cost of a believable promise |
| A plain, text-only product | Nothing to download is also nothing to build. Polish comes later |
| Founders covering dispatch gaps by hand in the first zone | It keeps promises while the software learns |

| Never trade for speed | Why |
|---|---|
| Accuracy of claims | No invented proof, arrival times, or counts (both briefs, Section 5). A growth story built on a made-up number fails at scale |
| Safety triage before dispatch | Gas, fire, or injury go to 911 and the utility first (S60) |
| Paying contractors on time | It is the reason they joined |
| Refunding customers automatically | It is the reason they trust the guarantee |
| Privacy as promised | One breach of "only the contractor who accepts sees your address" undoes VP2 |
| Messaging and email law, and ad policy | Marketing texts need written consent (S31); cold email follows CAN-SPAM (S73); Google's rules on message assets and local services (S70 to S72) |

## 5. Unit economics at speed

The engine on the customer side is paid search, and it is expensive. Plumbing search ads ran about $10.49 a click with 7.63 percent of clicks becoming tracked leads in 2025 (S15, B): about $137 per lead computed from those averages, against a reported $129. One agency's emergency plumbing data shows about $66.82 a click and 31 percent click-to-lead (S69, C), about $216 per lead.

For SOS SMS, a lead is a first text, and not every first text becomes a paid job. Cost per paid job from paid search:

**Paid acquisition cost per paid job = cost per click ÷ click-to-text rate ÷ text-to-paid rate**

Illustrative only, with a text-to-paid rate of 50 percent (the planning value, R30, replaced by the measured rate once a zone runs):

| Basis | Cost per first text | Per paid job at 50 percent | At 70 percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plumbing search benchmark (S15) | about $137 | about $275 | about $196 |
| Emergency plumbing dataset (S69) | about $216 | about $431 | about $308 |

Those figures sit well above typical trip charges (about $75 to $100 when charged, S28) and after-hours service call fees ($150 to $250, S50). **A customer fee alone will not pay back paid search on the first job.** That is normal for a marketplace buying liquidity, and it sets the four levers that make the model work:

| Lever | Effect | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Owned share of requests | Blended cost falls as more requests come from saved numbers, shares, and organic pages | Blended cost per paid job = (1 - owned share) × paid cost per paid job. At 50 percent owned, $275 becomes about $137 |
| 2. Jobs per customer | Repeat emergencies and more trades behind one number spread the cost | Cost per job = cost per customer ÷ lifetime jobs. At 1.5 jobs, $275 becomes about $183 |
| 3. Cheaper paid sources | Microsoft Ads clicks are commonly 30 to 60 percent cheaper (S74, C); long-tail problem keywords cost less than "emergency plumber near me" | Test both in every proving zone |
| 4. Revenue from both sides | Contractors pay $125 to $300 in lead spend per booked job on per-lead platforms at a 20 percent close rate (Run 1 Phase 9). A per-completed-job contractor fee below about $125 still saves them money | Take per job = customer fee + contractor fee, after the founding period: $49 + $25 = $74 |

**Decided, revised for paid demand (decision 2):** $0 to contractors during the founding period, as Run 1 recommends, and a $25 per-completed-job contractor fee after it, far below the Run 1 ceiling and written into the founding agreement; founding contractors keep $0 a job for 12 months after the founding period. The customer pays a $49 SOS SMS fee per dispatched request. Treat paid search as the liquidity engine of each new zone and owned channels as the profit engine of each mature zone. The live calculator is in Run 2's `pricing_dashboard.xlsx`, Calculators sheet, section 3.

**Funding implication.** Paid search during proving periods is a cash cost before the economics close, so runway of at least 18 months at Stage A spending (R35) has to cover several zones' proving periods at once. Raise for the plan's spending, with margin for it taking longer than hoped.

## 6. Stages of scale, and what changes at each

Organizations change shape as they grow, and what works at one size breaks at the next. Stages here are by headcount.

| Stage | Team | Zones | What the founders do | Hires that make the next stage possible | How decisions get made |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Under 10 people | One zone, one trade | Everything: product, ads, contractor support, dispatch backup. Both founders work supply in Stage 1; at Stage 2 one becomes the supply lead and the other the demand lead (R25) | A paid search operator; a product engineer who owns dispatch and payments | Founders decide everything, daily |
| B | Tens | Several zones, then a second metro | Design the zone template and recruiting kit; stop doing each zone by hand | Supply growth (email and partners); trust, safety, and payments operations; analytics | Small teams own a metric each; weekly review against the zone signals |
| C | Hundreds | Many metros | Allocate capital across metros; hire leaders | Metro launch teams; compliance; regional operations | Leaders own metros; the founders own the plan |
| D and E | Thousands and beyond | National, many trades | Out of scope for this plan | | |

Rules that go with the stages:

- **Hire for the next stage, not forever.** The right paid search operator for one zone is not necessarily the right head of growth for twenty.
- **Accept imperfect management early.** Process that would be overhead at Stage A becomes necessary at Stage B. Add it then, not before.
- **Change the playbook when the stage changes.** What worked in the first zone (founders stepping in) becomes a liability in the tenth.

## 7. The counterintuitive rules, applied to SOS SMS

| Rule | Applied |
|---|---|
| Ship before it is polished | Launch text-only, no app, minimal website, one trade. But not before the four things that must work on day one: guarantee terms, safety triage, carrier registration (S32), and payouts |
| Choose which problems to leave unfixed | Leave: website polish, a second trade, a membership, non-emergency scheduling, broad content. Never leave: refunds, payouts, safety, claim accuracy, privacy |
| Do things that do not scale, in the first zone only | Founders reconcile payouts and cover dispatch gaps by hand. Each manual task gets a date by which software replaces it |
| Listen to customers selectively | Build what shortens the path from first text to arrival. Defer requests outside it, however reasonable (scheduled maintenance, quotes for remodels) |
| Raise more than the plan needs | Paid search proving costs come before the economics close (section 5) |
| Keep the culture deliberate as it grows | Write down the non-negotiables in section 4 now, so new hires inherit them |

## 8. When to accelerate, and when to slow down

| Gate | Condition to pass | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Zone proven | Every section 3 signal at its threshold for 4 consecutive weeks | Accelerate in the zone: full covered hours, paid search budget raised to the point where acceptance holds |
| 2. Zone full | Covered hours at the guarantee standard; owned share rising | Open the adjacent zone with the template |
| 3. Metro proven | Three zones pass Gate 1 with the template, without founders stepping in | Open a second metro with a metro launch team |
| 4. Trade added | Plumbing density holds; a referred trade reaches its own density | A second trade behind the same number |

**Slow down or stop** in a zone when SOS-caused refunds, acceptance, or contractor activity cross their thresholds, or when paid cost per paid job rises above the ceiling (decision 12: $275 while proving, $192 by the end of month 6, $137 by the end of month 12). Speed is a choice made zone by zone, not a commitment that cannot be reversed.

## 9. Guardrails for scaling responsibly

- Nothing invented, ever. Pre-launch figures are planning values with their basis, never results; proof needs permission (both briefs).
- Safety triage before dispatch (cross-side check, item F).
- Contractors' terms in writing, before sign-up; payouts on time.
- Privacy as promised; no ad retargeting.
- Compliance: TCPA consent for marketing texts (S31); CAN-SPAM for contractor email (S73); Google ad policies (S70 to S72), including never presenting SOS SMS as the plumber.
- Fair treatment of contractors in the late-arrival rule (Run 1 Phase 9, option C).

## 10. Risks of scaling this fast

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Paid search never pays back | Owned share flat; cost per paid job above the ceiling | Slow paid spend; push owned loops, Microsoft Ads, long-tail keywords; bring forward the contractor fee |
| Dependence on one ad platform | Policy change or disapproval (S70 to S72) | Keep Microsoft Ads and organic pages live in every zone from the start |
| Thin zones break the guarantee | Refunds rise at night or in freezes | Narrow covered hours; recruit for those hours before buying more ads |
| Incumbents respond | Quote marketplaces add arrival guarantees | Density is the defense: a guarantee needs contractors on the ground |
| Contractors multi-home and get poached | Contractor activity falls | Payout speed, fair late-arrival rule, the founding terms |
