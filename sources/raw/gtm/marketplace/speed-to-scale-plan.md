# Speed-to-scale plan

How SOS SMS grows once a zone works: product-led, with no sales team, and with speed chosen over efficiency where the market rewards it. This plan applies a set of scaling principles to SOS SMS's two-sided, local marketplace. It sits on top of both runs and changes no claim in them.

The principles, in one paragraph: in a market where the advantage goes to whoever reaches scale first rather than whoever launches first, a company may deliberately accept inefficiency, in spending, in process, in polish, to grow faster than the competition. That bet only makes sense when the business model can pay off at scale, and it has to be managed in stages, with guardrails, and with a clear sense of when to stop.

Every SOS SMS number below is a decided value, a planning value with its basis, or labeled illustrative, from `resolution-register.md`; none is a result. Market figures carry source IDs from `../research/SOURCES.md`.

**Revised 2026-10-11, the membership revision.** The demand side is a monthly or annual membership, joined before the need, so the engine of each zone is now partner-led and owned acquisition before anything breaks (affiliates, email, the launch list), with paid search and social for sign-ups only where they come in under the acquisition ceiling. Sections 1 to 5, 7, 8, 9, and 10 carry the revised economics; the contractor side is unchanged.

## 1. Does speed decide the winner in this market?

Speed is worth paying for when a market is large, has network effects, and is being contested now. SOS SMS meets the first two conditions in a specific way:

| Condition | SOS SMS | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Large market | Yes. 85.7 million owner-occupied homes (S62, A); water damage and freezing claims at about 1 in 60 insured homes a year (S57, B); about 504,500 plumbing jobs on the supply side (S23, A) | Room for a large business, once emergency trades beyond plumbing are added |
| Network effects | Yes, but **local**. More contractors in a zone means faster acceptance, which wins more members there, which keeps contractors busy there. A contractor in one metro does nothing for a member in another | The race is run zone by zone and metro by metro, not nationally |
| Lock-in | Weak on the supply side: contractors also use Angi, Thumbtack, and Google (S03 to S08). Stronger on the member side: a membership renews and the number is already saved, and monthly churn measures it (R59) | Speed buys density. Loyalty has to come from the product: the saved number, the trip paid before rolling, the guarantee, and a membership worth keeping in the months nothing breaks |
| Contested now | Partly. Plumber clubs, warranties, and utility-partner plans are established, and HomeServe alone has more than 1,300 partners (S93); no one in the researched set offers SOS SMS's combination (`../research/`) | There is a window to reach density, and members, first in chosen zones |

**Conclusion.** Grow fast **inside** a zone once it is proven, and fast **across** zones only once the zone playbook repeats with predictable economics. Before a zone is proven, grow carefully and learn.

## 3. The two growth constraints

### Product and market fit, proven per zone

Not proven anywhere yet. The signals below decide whether a zone has it. The thresholds are set **before** launch (resolution register, R32), so the result cannot be argued after the fact.

| Signal | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance | Share of requests accepted inside the remaining window | 90 percent or more |
| Arrival | Share of accepted jobs arriving inside the window | 95 percent or more |
| SOS-caused refunds | Refunds SOS SMS pays, per 100 requests | 5 or fewer |
| Monthly churn | Share of monthly members who cancel in a month | 6.5 percent or less (R59) |
| Cost per new paid member | Acquisition spend, affiliate commissions included, ÷ new paid members, by channel | At or under the ceiling, about $43 (R63) |
| Contractor activity | Share of contractors accepting at least one job in the last 30 days | 60 percent or more |

### Operational scalability, without a sales or operations army

A product-led company with no sales team scales only what the product does on its own. Everything a person does in the first zone has to become software, or a repeatable kit, before the zone after next.

| Operation | First zone | Before scaling across zones |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor verification | Founders check licenses and insurance by hand | License and insurance checks in onboarding; recheck reminders |
| Dispatch | Software, with founders watching and stepping in | Software only; alerts when acceptance falls |
| Payment hold, release, payout | Payment provider, founders reconcile | Automated; exceptions queue |
| Guarantee refunds | Paid automatically from day one | Same |
| Disputes | Founders handle | Rules plus a small queue |
| Safety triage | Automated reply, founders review | Automated, audited |
| Membership billing, renewals, cancellations | Payment provider; founders handle exceptions | Automated, with renewal reminders and an exceptions queue |
| Affiliates | Signed by the demand lead; codes issued by hand | The affiliate program page, the kit, and generated codes and statements |
| Sign-ups and the launch list | Built by hand for one zone | A zone template: the launch list sequence, the opening email, affiliate codes, sign-up ad groups, the membership page |
| Contractor recruiting | Cold email and one supply house | A zone recruiting kit: list build, sequences, partner pack |

## 4. Speed over efficiency: where to spend to go faster, and where never to

| Accept inefficiency here | Why it is worth it |
|---|---|
| Affiliate commissions and sign-up spend up to the ceiling in proving zones | They buy members fast, and with them the answers that matter: acceptance by hour, refunds, churn. Waiting for word of mouth alone would take seasons |
| $0 to contractors during the founding period | Supply is the constrained side; every contractor added raises acceptance for every member in the zone |
| Recruiting more contractors than the density model asks for | Buffers nights, weekends, and freeze spikes, when everyone is busy at once |
| Paying guarantee refunds generously and automatically | Each refund is the cost of a believable promise |
| A plain, text-only product | Nothing to download is also nothing to build. Polish comes later |
| Founders covering dispatch gaps by hand in the first zone | It keeps promises while the software learns |

| Never trade for speed | Why |
|---|---|
| Accuracy of claims | No invented proof, arrival times, or counts (both briefs, Section 5). A growth story built on a made-up number fails at scale |
| Safety triage before dispatch | Gas, fire, or injury go to 911 and the utility first (S60) |
| Paying contractors on time | It is the reason they joined |
| Refunding members automatically | It is the reason they trust the guarantee |
| Easy cancellation | Cancel any time, on the membership page or by texting HELP (R56); a membership that is hard to leave breaks the law and the trust (S87) |
| Privacy as promised | One breach of "only the contractor who accepts sees your address" undoes VP2 |
| Messaging, email, subscription, and affiliate law, and ad policy | Marketing texts need written consent (S31); all email follows CAN-SPAM (S73); automatic renewal terms and consent (S87); affiliates disclose payment (S88); no payments at a home purchase settlement (S86, S94); Google's rules on local services (S70, S71) |

## 5. Unit economics at speed

The engine on the member side is the membership: a member pays every month or year, before anything breaks, and the contribution left after costs is what acquisition is paid from. Illustrative, from the pricing model at the planning values (R51 to R64):

| Measure | Monthly plan at $10 | Annual plan at $100 |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution, founding period | $8.51 a month | about $85.97 a year, about $7.16 a month |
| Contribution after the founding period, with the $25 contractor fee | $9.03 a month | about $92.22 a year |
| Churn or renewal | 6.5 percent a month (R59) | 75 percent renewal (R60) |
| First 6 months of contribution | about $43, the acquisition ceiling (R63) | paid up front |
| Lifetime contribution | about $131 | about $85.97 a year while renewed |

**Acquisition cost per new paid member = channel cost ÷ new paid members from it**, by channel and zone, with affiliate commissions counted in it.

| Channel | Cost per new paid member | Against the ceiling of about $43 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliates | $20, the commission (R61) | Under | Market range $4 to $56 an enrollment (S85, C) |
| Email, SOS SMS's own list | Counted from the first opening email | Measured | Open and click benchmarks only (S92, C) |
| Paid search, plumbing benchmark | about $137: $10.49 a click, 7.63 percent converting, assumed equal for sign-ups | Over, about 3 times | S15, B |
| Paid search, Microsoft, 30 to 60 percent cheaper clicks | about $55 to $96 | Over | S74, C |

**Paid search at plumbing benchmarks will not pay back a new member inside 6 months.** Affiliates and the launch list do, which is why they lead and paid sign-ups support. Four levers make the model work:

| Lever | Effect | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Partner-led acquisition | Affiliates and email bring members in under the ceiling, before the need | Cost per new paid member = commission or send cost ÷ joins |
| 2. Retention | Every point of monthly churn removed raises what a member is worth | First 6 months = contribution a month × (1 - (1 - churn)^6) ÷ churn; lifetime = contribution a month ÷ churn |
| 3. Annual plans and more trades | Annual members pay up front and renew at about 75 percent (R60); more trades behind the same membership give a member more reasons to stay | Contribution a year × years kept |
| 4. Revenue from both sides | After the founding period, $25 per completed paid job from contractors adds about $0.52 a member a month at 0.25 requests a member a year (R58) | $25 × 0.25 ÷ 12 |

**Decided (decision 2, revised 2026-10-11):** members pay a monthly or annual membership, planning values $10 a month or $100 a year (R51, R52), with no fee per request; $0 to contractors during the founding period and a $25 per-completed-job contractor fee after it, far below the Run 1 ceiling and written into the founding agreement; founding contractors keep $0 a job for 12 months after the founding period. Treat affiliates and the launch list as the engine that opens each zone, and paid sign-ups as support that runs only under the ceiling. The live calculator is in Run 2's `pricing_dashboard.xlsx`, Calculators sheet.

**Funding implication.** Members join before the need, but a zone cannot open until supply reaches density, so each zone's first months go to recruiting contractors and growing its launch list before any membership revenue arrives. Runway of at least 18 months at Stage A spending (R35) has to cover several zones' proving periods at once. Raise for the plan's spending, with margin for it taking longer than hoped.

## 6. Stages of scale, and what changes at each

Organizations change shape as they grow, and what works at one size breaks at the next. Stages here are by headcount.

| Stage | Team | Zones | What the founders do | Hires that make the next stage possible | How decisions get made |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Under 10 people | One zone, one trade | Everything: product, affiliates, email, contractor support, dispatch backup. Both founders work supply first in Stage 1, while the demand lead signs affiliates and grows the launch list; at Stage 2 one becomes the supply lead and the other the demand lead (R25) | A partnerships and lifecycle email operator; a product engineer who owns dispatch, payments, and membership billing | Founders decide everything, daily |
| B | Tens | Several zones, then a second metro | Design the zone template and recruiting kit; stop doing each zone by hand | Supply growth (email and partners); member partnerships at metro scale (utilities, cities, associations); trust, safety, payments, and billing operations; analytics | Small teams own a metric each; weekly review against the zone signals |
| C | Hundreds | Many metros | Allocate capital across metros; hire leaders | Metro launch teams; compliance; regional operations | Leaders own metros; the founders own the plan |
| D and E | Thousands and beyond | National, many trades | Out of scope for this plan | | |

Rules that go with the stages:

- **Hire for the next stage, not forever.** The right partnerships operator for one zone is not necessarily the right head of growth for twenty.
- **Accept imperfect management early.** Process that would be overhead at Stage A becomes necessary at Stage B. Add it then, not before.
- **Change the playbook when the stage changes.** What worked in the first zone (founders stepping in) becomes a liability in the tenth.

## 7. The counterintuitive rules, applied to SOS SMS

| Rule | Applied |
|---|---|
| Ship before it is polished | Launch text-only, no app, minimal website, one trade. But not before the four things that must work on day one: guarantee terms, safety triage, carrier registration (S32), and payouts |
| Choose which problems to leave unfixed | Leave: website polish, a second trade, non-emergency scheduling, broad content. Never leave: refunds, payouts, safety, claim accuracy, privacy, easy cancellation |
| Do things that do not scale, in the first zone only | Founders reconcile payouts and cover dispatch gaps by hand. Each manual task gets a date by which software replaces it |
| Listen to members selectively | Build what shortens the path from the request text to arrival, and what keeps the membership worth keeping. Defer requests outside it, however reasonable (scheduled maintenance, quotes for remodels) |
| Raise more than the plan needs | Supply is recruited and the launch list grown before any membership revenue arrives (section 5) |
| Keep the culture deliberate as it grows | Write down the non-negotiables in section 4 now, so new hires inherit them |

## 8. When to accelerate, and when to slow down

| Gate | Condition to pass | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Zone proven | Every section 3 signal at its threshold for 4 consecutive weeks | Accelerate in the zone: full covered hours; affiliate and sign-up spend raised while cost per new paid member holds under the ceiling and acceptance holds |
| 2. Zone full | Covered hours at the guarantee standard; members growing toward about 430 (R64) | Open the adjacent zone with the template |
| 3. Metro proven | Three zones pass Gate 1 with the template, without founders stepping in | Open a second metro with a metro launch team |
| 4. Trade added | Plumbing density holds; a referred trade reaches its own density | A second trade behind the same membership |

**Slow down or stop** in a zone when SOS-caused refunds, acceptance, monthly churn, or contractor activity cross their thresholds, or when a channel's cost per new paid member rises above the ceiling (decision 12: about $43, R63). Speed is a choice made zone by zone, not a commitment that cannot be reversed.

## 9. Guardrails for scaling responsibly

- Nothing invented, ever. Pre-launch figures are planning values with their basis, never results; proof needs permission (both briefs).
- Safety triage before dispatch (cross-side check, item F).
- Contractors' terms in writing, before sign-up; payouts on time.
- Privacy as promised; no ad retargeting.
- Compliance: TCPA consent for marketing texts (S31); CAN-SPAM for all email (S73); ROSCA and state automatic renewal laws for the membership (S87); FTC Endorsement Guides for affiliates (S88); RESPA, no payments at a home purchase settlement (S86, S94); Google ad policies (S70, S71), including never presenting SOS SMS as the plumber.
- Fair treatment of contractors in the late-arrival rule (Run 1 Phase 9, option C).

## 10. Risks of scaling this fast

| Risk | Signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition costs more than a member is worth | A channel's cost per new paid member above the ceiling (R63) | Pause that channel; shift spend to affiliates and the launch list; push annual plans; bring forward the contractor fee |
| Members leave in quiet months | Monthly churn above 6.5 percent (R59) | The readiness kit, seasonal notes, annual plans, and renewal reminders that say plainly what renews; never make cancelling hard (S87) |
| Dependence on one partner or one ad platform | A partner ends its program, or an ad policy changes (S70, S71) | Several affiliates per zone, SOS SMS's own email list, and organic pages live from the start |
| Too few requests for contractors | Members per zone far below about 430 (R64); contractor activity falls | Grow the launch list and affiliates before a zone opens; recruit to the density number, not past it |
| Thin zones break the guarantee | Refunds rise at night or in freezes | Narrow covered hours; recruit for those hours before opening more sign-ups |
| Incumbents respond | Plumber clubs, warranties, or quote marketplaces add an arrival guarantee | Density is the defense: a guarantee needs contractors on the ground |
| Contractors multi-home and get poached | Contractor activity falls | Payout speed, fair late-arrival rule, the founding terms |
