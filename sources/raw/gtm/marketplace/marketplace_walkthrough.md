# SLIDE 1, SOS SMS Marketplace GTM: What the Two Dashboard Suites Are For

- SOS SMS is a pre-launch, two-sided home repair network: homeowners join as members before anything breaks, and in an emergency a member texts one number; licensed and insured contractors accept
- Two full GTM runs, one per side, never blended: contractors first, members second, product-led with no sales team
- Contractor promise: no charge to see a request, and the trip paid before you roll
- Member promise: one text, and help is on the way; a missed 90-minute window costs SOS SMS money
- So what: every metric is a planning value until a zone runs, and supply density decides where memberships can open

---

# SLIDE 2, Product Feature Matrix Dashboards: What Each Side Leads With

- Contractors: the trip charge collected before rolling scored highest on competitiveness; "no lead fees" is wanted by all but not unique
- Members: one text starts dispatch, because the card and address went on file at joining; the 90-minute guarantee and the arrival time stand with it
- Every "Unique To Us" rating on a named item in the source matrix failed either the skill's validation rule or the research
- On the member side, a price range before arrival is wanted by all; it is not offered at launch (R49), and the $99 trip charge is stated before joining instead
- So what: lead with being paid to show up on one side and one text with a guaranteed window on the other

---

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis Dashboards: Who Each Side Weighs Us Against

- Contractors compare Angi Leads, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads, Service Direct, warranty networks, and word of mouth
- Members compare doing nothing in advance, plumber clubs, home warranties, utility-partner plans, Frontdoor, and on-demand marketplaces
- Contractors complain about paying for leads that never become jobs and about shared requests
- Homeowners complain about unanswered calls at night, slow claims, waiting periods, surprise fees, and floods of calls
- So what: the same dispatch design answers both: contractors pay nothing to see a request, and only one contacts the member

# SLIDE 4, Positioning Dashboards: Four Vectors per Side, Current and Projected

- Contractor side, we lead projected: no charge for requests that never become work, and paid to show up
- Member side, we lead projected: one text with a money-backed arrival window, and many contractors reached with one reply
- They lead: volume and exclusivity for contractors; paying for repairs and a known brand for members
- Current state on every vector is 0, because nothing is live
- So what: density in a zone is both the constraint and the moat

# SLIDE 5, ICP Dashboards: Who We Win First on Each Side

- Contractors first: small plumbing and water damage shops already paying per lead, with on-call capacity
- Owner-operators alongside, to fill hours and refer other trades
- Members: new homeowners without a go-to plumber, and established homeowners whose plumber does not answer at night
- Reach members before the need, through affiliates and email, with search and social for sign-ups in open zones
- So what: recruit the shops that can cover nights, then open memberships only where they can

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee Dashboards: Personas, Blockers, Drivers

- Contractor shops: technician, dispatcher, owner; the dispatcher is the likely blocker
- Members: a household of one or two adults deciding over days; "another subscription" is the blocker
- The founding contractor cohort is sized by a density model: 9 plumbing contractors at a planned acceptance rate of 0.30, measured by hour band in Stage 1
- The founding member area is the neighborhoods inside that cohort's coverage, with about 430 members as the planning value for a zone (R64)
- So what: both first impressions are written documents: the terms on one page and the membership page

---

# SLIDE 7, Customer Journey Funnel Dashboards: Two Journeys, One Hand-off

- Contractor value realization is the first paid trip; member value realization is arrival inside the window, weeks or months after joining
- The hand-off between them is acceptance: an arrival time inside the remaining window, or no acceptance at all
- Shared KPIs: time from the request text to acceptance, share arriving within the window, SOS-caused refund share
- Blockers on both sides are infrastructure: payout setup, carrier registration, membership billing, a card that fails
- So what: refunds paid automatically and payouts on time are what make both promises believable

# SLIDE 8, Lead Magnets: One per Side

- Contractors: the Job Leak Scorecard, scoring lead spend, close rate, unpaid trips, and missed calls from their own answers
- Members: How Ready Is Your Home for a 2 a.m. Leak, ending at the ZIP check: join, the launch list, or the plan alone
- Neither uses case studies or projections, because none exist yet
- Both are self-serve: the Lead Cost Check for contractors, the readiness scorecard at the top of SOS SMS's own email list for members
- So what: each side's first contact starts with something useful, not a pitch

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging Dashboards: The Take Rate

- Decided: members pay a monthly or annual membership, planning values $10 a month or $100 a year, plus the $99 trip charge on use, credited to the repair; no fee per request
- Contractors pay $0 in the founding period, then $25 per completed paid job, far below the cheapest per-lead alternative's cost per booked job
- Late-arrival rule: fault-based, tied to the arrival time the contractor commits to inside the window from the member's request text; the refund is the $99 trip charge
- About $8.51 a member a month of contribution sets an acquisition ceiling of about $43 per new paid member: affiliates fit under it, paid search at plumbing benchmarks does not
- The twelve decisions, listed in the cross-side check, were made on 2026-10-03 and revised by the membership revision on 2026-10-11
- So what: members fund the network, contractors join free, and acquisition before the need decides how fast each zone fills
