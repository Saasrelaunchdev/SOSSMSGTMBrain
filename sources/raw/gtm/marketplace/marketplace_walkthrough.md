# SLIDE 1, SOS SMS Marketplace GTM: What the Two Dashboard Suites Are For

- SOS SMS is a pre-launch, two-sided marketplace for emergency home repair: customers text one number, licensed and insured contractors accept
- Two full GTM runs, one per side, never blended: contractors first, customers second, product-led with no sales team
- Contractor promise: no charge to see a request, and the trip paid before you roll
- Customer promise: one text reaches the vetted contractors nearby, and a missed 90-minute window costs SOS SMS money
- So what: every metric is a planning value until a zone runs, and supply density decides where the customer side can open

---

# SLIDE 2, Product Feature Matrix Dashboards: What Each Side Leads With

- Contractors: the trip charge collected before rolling scored highest on competitiveness; "no lead fees" is wanted by all but not unique
- Customers: the 90-minute guarantee scored highest on both axes; text-only and privacy are mechanics, not "only we"
- Every "Unique To Us" rating on a named item in the source matrix failed either the skill's validation rule or the research
- New finding on the customer side: a price range before arrival is wanted by all; it is not offered at launch (R49), and the $99 trip charge is stated before dispatch instead
- So what: lead with being paid to show up on one side and the guaranteed window on the other

---

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis Dashboards: Who Each Side Weighs Us Against

- Contractors compare Angi Leads, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads, Service Direct, warranty networks, and word of mouth
- Customers compare searching and calling, Roto-Rooter, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Angi, Thumbtack, and the warranty line
- Contractors complain about paying for leads that never become jobs and about shared requests
- Customers complain about no-shows, unanswered calls at night, surprise fees, and floods of calls
- So what: the same dispatch design answers both: contractors pay nothing to see a request, and only one contacts the customer

# SLIDE 4, Positioning Dashboards: Four Vectors per Side, Current and Projected

- Contractor side, we lead projected: no charge for requests that never become work, and paid to show up
- Customer side, we lead projected: guaranteed arrival window, and many contractors reached with one reply
- They lead: volume and exclusivity for contractors; brand and guarantees on the work for customers
- Current state on every vector is 0, because nothing is live
- So what: density in a zone is both the constraint and the moat

# SLIDE 5, ICP Dashboards: Who We Win First on Each Side

- Contractors first: small plumbing and water damage shops already paying per lead, with on-call capacity
- Owner-operators alongside, to fill hours and refer other trades
- Customers: homeowners inside the founding zone, with no go-to contractor or one who does not answer at night
- Reach customers in the moment, through bottom-of-funnel search ads and problem pages
- So what: recruit the shops that can cover nights, then advertise only where they can

---

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee Dashboards: Personas, Blockers, Drivers

- Contractor shops: technician, dispatcher, owner; the dispatcher is the likely blocker
- Customers: one person deciding in minutes; the payment link from an unknown number is the blocker
- The founding contractor cohort is sized by a density model: 9 plumbing contractors at a planned acceptance rate of 0.30, measured by hour band in Stage 1
- The founding customer area is the neighborhoods inside that cohort's coverage
- So what: both first impressions are written documents: the terms on one page and the first reply text

---

# SLIDE 7, Customer Journey Funnel Dashboards: Two Journeys, One Hand-off

- Contractor value realization is the first paid trip; customer value realization is arrival inside the window
- The hand-off between them is acceptance: an arrival time inside the remaining window, or no acceptance at all
- Shared KPIs: time to acceptance, share arriving within the window, SOS-caused refund share
- Blockers on both sides are infrastructure: payout setup, carrier registration, link trust, card availability
- So what: refunds paid automatically and payouts on time are what make both promises believable

# SLIDE 8, Lead Magnets: One per Side

- Contractors: the Job Leak Scorecard, scoring lead spend, close rate, unpaid trips, and missed calls from their own answers
- Customers: How Ready Is Your Home for a 2 a.m. Leak, ending with Text READY to save the number
- Neither uses case studies or projections, because none exist yet
- Both lead into a founder: an audit call for contractors, a saved contact card for customers
- So what: each side's first contact starts with something useful, not a pitch

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging Dashboards: The Take Rate

- Decided: customers pay a $99 trip charge credited to the repair plus a $49 SOS SMS fee; contractors pay $0 in the founding period, then $25 per completed paid job
- Late-arrival rule: fault-based, tied to the arrival time the contractor commits to inside the window; the refund is the $99 trip charge
- Paid search runs about $137 to $216 per first text on plumbing benchmarks, so revenue from both sides and a rising owned share are what close the economics
- The contractor fee is per completed paid job, far below the cheapest per-lead alternative's cost per booked job
- The twelve decisions, listed in the cross-side check, were made on 2026-10-03 and unblock both runs
- So what: SOS SMS takes $49 per completed job in the founding period and $74 after it, and the owned share decides when paid search pays back
