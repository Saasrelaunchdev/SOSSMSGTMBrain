# Positioning summary, contractor side

SOS SMS can lead the contractor side of its category by focusing on two vectors the incumbents cannot easily copy: no charge for requests that never become work, and being paid to show up. Every lead platform in the set earns its revenue when a contractor receives a request, so none of them can stop charging for requests without changing its business model, and none of them handles the payment for the visit. That is where contractor acquisition comes from: a contractor who has paid for leads that never replied (the most repeated complaint in the research) has a concrete reason to try a platform where seeing a request costs nothing and the truck leaves with the trip already paid. The pitch is mechanics, not volume, which suits a pre-launch platform that has no volume to show.

Growth is bounded by geography and density, not by demand from contractors. Each new metro and trade needs its own first cohort, and memberships cannot open in a place until that cohort exists, so supply growth sets the pace for the whole marketplace. Defensibility comes from local density rather than technology: once a metro has enough active contractors in each of the three trades for the arrival time and the 90-minute guarantee to be honest, a new entrant would need the same density before it could make the same promise to customers. The weak point to manage is shared requests. Every matched contractor sees each job, which contractors associate with the platforms they dislike, and the answer has to be stated up front: on SOS SMS, a request you do not win costs you nothing.
