# SLIDE 1, SOS SMS Contractor GTM: What the Dashboard Suite Is For

- SOS SMS is a pre-launch, two-sided marketplace for emergency home repair, and this deck covers the contractor side
- Nine dashboards plus a lead magnet turn one question into a launch plan: why would a licensed plumber try one more platform?
- The answer the suite builds toward: no charge to see a request, and the trip paid before you roll
- Every SOS SMS metric is a planning value until the founding cohort produces real data
- So what: supply is the constrained side, and memberships cannot open in a zone until this side reaches density

---

# SLIDE 2, Product Feature Matrix Dashboard: What We Learned and What We Lead With

- Hero features: the trip charge collected before the contractor rolls, no lead fees, and emergency-only requests
- The trip charge scored highest on competitiveness: no lead platform in the researched set collects it for the contractor
- "No lead fees" is wanted by all but not unique, and the source matrix's "Unique" rating failed the skill's own validation rule
- Table stakes: matching by trade and area, decline freely, keeping the customer
- So what: lead with being paid to show up, open the door with no lead fees

---

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis Dashboard: Competitor Map and Buying Context

- Six alternatives: Angi Leads, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads, Service Direct, home warranty networks, and word of mouth
- Contractors praise volume and exclusive calls, and complain most about paying for leads that never become jobs
- The second complaint is shared requests, and SOS SMS also sends each request to every matched contractor
- The answer: on SOS SMS, a request you do not win costs you nothing
- So what: contractors buy on mechanics, so the terms on one page are the sales asset

---

# SLIDE 4, Positioning Dashboard: Four Vectors, Current State and Projected State

- They lead: lead volume and reach (Google LSA, Angi, Thumbtack) and exclusive calls (status quo, Service Direct)
- We lead, projected: no charge for requests that never become work (9.4 of 10) and paid to show up (9.1 of 10)
- Current state: 0 on every vector, because nothing is live
- Positioning line: the emergency dispatch line where seeing a request costs nothing and the trip is paid before you roll
- So what: SOS SMS wins contractors who already pay per lead, and does not pretend to beat exclusivity or volume

---

# SLIDE 5, ICP Dashboard: Who We Win First, and Why

- First: small plumbing and water damage shops that already pay for leads and run an on-call technician
- They have the clearest savings anchor and the night capacity the 90-minute member guarantee needs
- Alongside: owner-operators who rely on referrals, recruited to fill hours and refer other trades
- Triggers to watch: a lead platform renewal, a bad month of unreplied leads, freeze season
- So what: recruit the shops first, use owner-operators to close coverage gaps

---

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee Dashboard: Personas, Blockers, Drivers

- Shops: technician, dispatcher, owner. Owner-operators are all three at once
- The dispatcher is the likely blocker, because requests by text can go around the dispatch board
- Owners block on platform distrust and on the obligations: arrival commitments and crediting the trip charge
- Founding contractor cohort sized by a density model: 9 plumbing contractors at a planned acceptance rate of 0.30, measured in Stage 1
- So what: settle who receives requests before the first one arrives, and put every obligation in writing

---

# SLIDE 7, Customer Journey Funnel Dashboard: Stage Progression and Internal KPIs

- Self-serve end to end: cold email or a partner card, the Lead Cost Check, the terms on one page, sign-up by text
- Value realization is the first paid trip, tracked as days to first request and days to first paid trip
- Staying active is measured against the density target per trade, zone, and hour band
- Blockers: onboarding friction, payout timing, first-job silence, and the late-arrival rule
- So what: launch one dense zone instead of a thin metro, so no contractor joins to a silent phone

---

# SLIDE 8, Lead Magnet: The Job Leak Scorecard

- Seven questions, scored 0 to 100, on lead spend, close rate, unpaid trips, and missed after-hours calls
- Immediate value: the contractor's own cost per booked job, from their own answers
- High scores route to an automated lead spend report and sign-up by text, with no call
- No earnings projections and no case studies, because none exist yet
- So what: it starts every conversation with the contractor's numbers, not SOS SMS's pitch

---

# SLIDE 9, Assets and Collaterals Library Dashboard: Channel Coverage and TOFU to BOFU Flow

- Product-led, no sales team: cold email, a partnership program, and an in-product referral loop
- Top of funnel in every channel: the Lead Cost Check, in email, on a counter card, and forwardable
- Middle: the terms on one page and "how a job works in three texts"
- Bottom: sign-up by text, with the founding agreement and permission clause signed in the flow
- So what: every contractor signs themselves up, so the terms on one page have to answer every question a salesperson would have

---

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging Dashboard: Value Metric, Usage Metric, Tiers, Expansion Levers

- Contractors pay $0 during the founding period; a per-completed-job fee after it is planned from the start, never per request
- Usage metric for health, not price: accepted jobs per active contractor per month, by hour band
- Packaging: founding contractor and standard contractor, both free; paid priority flagged and not designed
- Late-arrival rule, decided: fault-based, tied to the arrival time the contractor commits to
- Expansion levers raise liquidity: more hours, more trades, more technicians, new zones at density
- So what: $0 to join and to see a request, $25 per completed paid job after the founding period, a fifth of the cheapest per-lead alternative's cost per booked job
