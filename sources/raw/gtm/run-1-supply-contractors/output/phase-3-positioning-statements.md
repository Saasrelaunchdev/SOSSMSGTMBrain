# Phase 3. Positioning Dashboard, contractor side

Three statements in the skill's template: against direct competitors, against indirect alternatives, and against the status quo. Each carries the conditions that must be true before it is used outside the company, because Phase 6 and the deck will lift these lines.

## Step 3.1. Positioning statements

**Against pay-per-lead platforms (Angi Leads, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads, Service Direct):**
Not "another lead service", a dispatch line for emergency jobs where you never pay to see a request. You accept the jobs you want, a request you do not win costs you nothing, and the customer's trip charge is collected before you roll.

- Must be true before use: no per-lead or per-request fee in the revenue model (decision 2 has none). The trip charge is released on arrival by the committed time, written in the terms on one page (decision 3).
- Answers the known weakness: every matched contractor sees each request. This statement makes the shared request harmless by saying exactly what it costs to lose one.
- Do not extend to: "exclusive", "guaranteed jobs", or any comparison of SOS SMS's cost to a named platform's fees without a dated source from Phase 2.

**Against indirect alternatives (answering services, warranty networks, running your own ads):**
Not "a cheaper answering service" or "a warranty network with less paperwork", a way to take emergency work at your own rates without paying for the phone to ring. There is no monthly fee, no one between you and the customer discounts your price for the work, and you set the hours you take requests.

- Must be true before use: contractors set their own price for the work, and no monthly fee exists (decision 2). SOS SMS sets the trip charge, $99 per trade and zone (decision 5), so never say "your price" about the trip charge.
- Anchor: answering services run about $250 to $720 a month for 50 to 200 minutes (S21, C); warranty networks pay set rates below private work (S20, C). Internal only at grade C.

**Against the status quo (word of mouth, your own listing, answering your own phone):**
Not "a replacement for your regulars", an overflow line for the emergencies you would take anyway, in the hours you choose. When it is quiet it costs nothing, and when you are booked you decline.

- Must be true before use: no subscription; declining carries no penalty (brief Section 5).
- This is the honest statement for owner-operators whose phone already rings. It does not ask them to change anything that works.

## Positioning in one line

For licensed plumbing and water-emergency contractors who are tired of paying for requests that never become work, SOS SMS is the emergency dispatch line where seeing a job costs nothing and the trip is paid before you roll.

## What this positioning deliberately does not claim

| Do not claim | Why |
|---|---|
| More jobs, a fuller schedule, or any volume | Pre-launch, no data, and an income claim (brief Section 5) |
| Exclusive requests | Every matched contractor sees each request (decision 4) |
| Better leads than a named competitor | No dated, sourced comparison exists yet |
| "Zero cost" without the revenue model stated in the same sentence | Becomes false if SOS SMS takes any share of the work; decision 2 takes none, so state the $25 per completed paid job fee alongside |
| "Guaranteed" trip charge without the late-arrival rule | The customer's 90-minute refund may pay out of it (brief Section 6, row 1) |
