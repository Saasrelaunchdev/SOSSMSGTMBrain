## Step 2.7. Review analysis

Done, see `../../research/supply-research.md`, Step 2.7. Summary of the themes used below:

- Lead platforms (C1 to C4) are praised for volume, reach, exclusivity (C4), and budget control (C3, C4).
- They are criticized for cost per lead, leads that never reply, and the same request going to several contractors (C1, C2), and for quality claims the FTC found unsubstantiated (C1).
- Warranty networks are criticized for rates and price control. The status quo is limited by volume and by the owner answering the phone at night.

**Scoring basis.** V1: national footprint and reach (S03, S05, S08, S10, S33). V2: whether the request reaches one contractor (S05, S10). SOS SMS scores low by design: every matched contractor sees each request. V3: whether a contractor pays for a request that does not become work (S03, S05, S08, S10). The status quo scores highest; SOS SMS's projected 9.4 holds under decision 2: the contractor fee is charged only on completed paid jobs, never on a request that does not become work. V4: whether payment for the visit is secured before the contractor drives out. Warranty networks pay for dispatched visits at set rates (S20); lead platforms do not touch payment.

**The scenario this creates.** SOS SMS is the obvious choice for one segment at this stage: contractors in the launch area who already pay per lead, take emergency calls, and want to stop paying for requests that never become paid trips. It is not the obvious choice for a contractor who wants exclusive calls (C4, C6) or maximum volume (C1, C3). The run should not pretend otherwise.

## Step 2.15. Category summary

**Vector 1: Lead volume and reach**
Leader: C3 Google LSA, closely followed by C1 Angi Leads and C2 Thumbtack.
Impact: owning the homeowner's search moment lets these platforms sell contractors a steady flow of requests. It acquires contractors easily, because "more leads" is a simple promise, and loses them when cost per booked job rises.

**Vector 2: Exclusive calls**
Leader: C6 Status quo, then C4 Service Direct.
Impact: exclusivity is what contractors say they want when they leave shared-lead platforms. Service Direct acquires contractors by being the exit from shared leads, at a premium price per call.

**Vector 3: No charge for requests that never become work**
Leader: SOS SMS, projected (9.4), alongside the status quo.
Impact: removes the contractor's main objection to any new platform, which is paying before knowing whether work will follow. Acquisition depends on contractors believing it, which is why the claim has to be narrow and the revenue model stated with it (decision 2: $25 per completed paid job, from the first job).

**Vector 4: Paid to show up**
Leader: SOS SMS, projected (9.1).
Impact: no lead platform touches the payment for the visit. Securing the trip charge before the contractor rolls is the most distinctive thing SOS SMS can say, and it is the reason for a contractor to try one more platform.
