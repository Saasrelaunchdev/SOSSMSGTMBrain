One text out. One yes back.

SOS SMS is a pre-launch, two-sided marketplace for emergency home repair. A person with a problem at home texts one number, and the request goes to licensed, insured contractors matched by trade and area. This book defines how the brand looks, moves, and speaks, so every surface says the same thing in the same calm voice.

01 · What the brand means

Every promise to a customer is kept by a contractor first.

That line carries the whole business. The customer's promises, one text that reaches people and an arrival window that costs SOS SMS money when it is missed, only hold where enough contractors have said yes. So the brand speaks to two people at once and never blends them.

Contractors

No charge to see a request, and the trip paid before you roll.

Licensed, insured trade contractors who take emergency calls. They join free, with no subscription and no lead fees.

Customers

One text, an arrival time when someone accepts, and a missed 90-minute window that costs SOS SMS money.

People with an urgent problem at home. The window's clock start, refund, and hours are [scope], waiting on a founder decision.

Sources: knowledge/GTM-REPO-README.md; marketplace_walkthrough.md, slide 1; specs/01, slide 01.
Calm under pressure

The subject is emergencies. The brand is not one.

Picture the room where requests are routed at 2 a.m.: dim, precise, quiet, legible at a glance. Nothing flashes, nothing alarms, nothing shouts.

Kept, not claimed

We say only what a contractor can deliver.

A promise goes out only where the other side's terms make it true. Until then it waits in brackets, visibly.

Fair to both sides

Money moves only to whoever it was promised to.

A request a contractor does not win costs them nothing. The trip charge reaches the one who accepts, and nobody else.

Exact, never inflated

Every number carries its source, or it does not appear.

SOS SMS is pre-launch. Unknowns show as {TBD}, never as a guess, a range, or "coming soon".

Sources: specs/02, 1.1 (dispatch-room calm); both GTM briefs, Section 6; Run 1 Phase 6, drill 2; knowledge/README.md, section 4.
02 · The mark

A sent message, with both sides inside it.

The bubble is the one number you text
An outgoing message: three round corners and one tight corner, the same shape as the message tile in the walkthrough. It holds both sides, because one number joins them.
The dot is the customer
Customers are always round, everywhere in the system: the origin of every request, the side badge, the launcher cards.
The square is the contractor
Contractors are always square. Shape travels with colour, so the two sides read apart even without colour.
Green is the yes
In the walkthrough, the one lit signal is the reply that comes back when a contractor accepts. Green means help is on its way, calmly. It never means alarm.

What the mark is never

Never red, never a siren, a cross, a lifebuoy, a bell, or a phone ringing. Never "911". SOS SMS is not the plumber and not an emergency service: it is the text that reaches the people who can come. For fire, a gas smell, or anyone hurt, call 911 first.

03 · Logo

Six lockups, one rule: always SOS SMS.

The name is always the full "SOS SMS". Several unrelated plumbing companies already use the first word alone, so it never appears on its own, in the logo or in copy.

Clear space: one contractor square

Keep at least the height of the square inside the mark clear on every side. The logo files already include this space.

Minimum size

Horizontal logo: 96 px wide on screen, 24 mm in print. Mark alone: 16 px. Below that, use the mark, not the lockup.

Don't recolour the mark red or orange. It is never an alarm.

Don't separate the two words, or use the first one alone.

Don't stretch, squash, tilt, or rotate the logo.

Don't add outlines, glows, shadows, or pulses.

07 · Motion

Objects arrive and settle. Signals travel. Nothing alarms.

One curve for every tween

cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1), the settle curve. It arrives quickly and eases to rest. No bounce, no overshoot, no spring, anywhere.

120 msMicro: presses, focus, toggles
240 msSmall: hovers, tooltips, chips
480 msStandard: text, tiles, bars
720 msLarge: columns, paths, phones
  • Projection looks different from fact: anything projected is drawn as an outline, never a filled solid.
  • Money moves only to whoever it was promised to, and only in the direction the terms say.
  • One ambient loop per slide, at most. Reduced motion shows every end state at once, and print shows it too.
08 · Voice

Plain, exact, unhurried.

Each claim stated once, with its source beside it. No hype and no urgency theatre: the reader may be standing in water.

Always

  • Write the full name, SOS SMS.
  • Write ranges with "to": $25 to $60.
  • Keep unknowns as {TBD} and decisions as [brackets].
  • Carry the safety line wherever copy invites an urgent text.
  • Show a grade C figure's grade, and keep it inside the company.

Never

  • Present SOS SMS as the plumber, an emergency service, or anything that reads as 911.
  • Use em dashes or en dashes, anywhere.
  • Use: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower.
  • Fill a gap with a plausible number, "for the demo".
  • Promise one side what the other side's terms do not deliver.
Lose the race, lose nothing.
