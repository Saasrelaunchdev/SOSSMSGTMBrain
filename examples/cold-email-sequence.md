# Example: cold email sequence (contractors)

Status: **DRAFT, not approved.** From Run 1 Phase 8 drafts, checked against the brain. Sends only after decisions 1 to 4 are logged, the terms on one page and the Lead Cost Check are live, and the sending setup is in place. Template: `templates/cold-email.md`.

Every email ends with: [Sender name], SOS SMS, [postal address]. Not interested? [one-click opt-out] (S73).

---

**Email 1, day 0. Subject: What did each job from your leads cost?**
> Hi [first name], one question for [business]: what did each booked job from your lead platforms cost last month, counting the leads that never answered?
>
> This two-minute check works it out from four numbers you already know: [link]. Your answers stay yours.
>
> We're building SOS SMS, emergency jobs by text in [zone], with no lead fees and the trip charge collected before you roll. [How SOS SMS is paid, one sentence.]

**Email 2, day 3. Subject: How a job works in three texts**
> Here is how a job will work once SOS SMS opens in [zone]. A customer texts what broke. Every matched contractor in the area sees it at once, and the first to accept gets the job. Everyone else pays nothing, because nobody pays to look.
>
> The customer will have paid the trip charge before you roll. [Image: the three texts, labeled as a mockup.] See how it works: [contractor page].

**Email 3, day 7. Subject: The terms, on one page**
> What you pay: [revenue model]. When you're paid: the trip charge is released when you arrive [release rule]. What you commit to: an arrival time when you accept, crediting the trip charge to the work if the customer goes ahead, and current license and insurance. If you're late: [late-arrival rule].
>
> All of it in writing: [terms link].

**Email 4, day 12. Subject: Founding contractors in [zone]**
> We're opening [zone] to customers once enough contractors have joined to cover it. Founding contractors get the first requests there and founding terms until [real deadline].
>
> To join, text JOIN to [number]; it takes about {TBD} minutes, with no call. Last email unless you reply.

---

## Why this works
- **Their number first.** Email 1 asks about the contractor's own cost and gives them a tool that is useful even if they never join. No claim about SOS SMS results.
- **The weak point, said plainly.** Email 2 states the shared request instead of hiding it, and makes it harmless.
- **Terms, not pitch.** Email 3 is what a burned contractor actually wants: what they pay, when they are paid, what they owe.
- **Pre-launch framing.** "We're building", "once SOS SMS opens", "will have paid": the design, never a working service (`rules/writing-rules.md`).
- **No false urgency.** Email 4 uses only a real deadline, then stops.
- "Request" for SOS SMS, "leads" only for the platforms the contractor already pays.
