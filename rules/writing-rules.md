# Writing rules

These apply to every word of output, on both sides of the marketplace, no exceptions.
last_reviewed: 2026-10-04

## Mechanics
- No em dashes and no en dashes, anywhere, including ranges. Write "$25 to $60" and "9 p.m. to 6 a.m.".
- Banned words: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower. Their variants too (unlocked, empowering, streamlined).
- Always the full name, SOS SMS. Never the first word alone, in copy, file names, ad keywords, or alt text.
- Short paragraphs, one to three sentences.
- Mechanics over adjectives: who pays what, when, and what happens if it goes wrong.
- No stacked adjectives, no exclamation marks, no hype, and no superlative that is not cleared in `do-not-say.md`.
- Every value comes from `reference/resolution-register.md`: decided, a planning value labelled as one, or counted at a named stage. Per-message details are `{{merge fields}}`, filled from a named record and never typed by hand. Nothing is left as a gap or a placeholder.
- Numbers carry their source ID (S01 to S74). A grade C figure stays internal and shows its grade. Grade A and B figures go into external copy only after someone opens the URL and confirms the figure on the page: the research was read from search summaries.

## Tone
Dispatch-room calm: plain, exact, unhurried, each claim stated once. The subject is emergencies; the copy is not one. The customer may be standing in water and needs the next step, not a pitch. The contractor has heard every platform pitch and trusts terms, not adjectives.

## Per side
- **Contractors.** Talk money and terms first: what they pay, when they are paid, what they commit to. Plain trade talk, no marketing gloss. The answer to "what's the catch" goes in the first screen.
- **Customers.** Value before payment: stop-the-water steps come before any offer. Who SOS SMS is comes before any link. The safety line whenever copy invites an urgent text.

## Writing for a text message
Text is the product. Every customer and contractor message follows these.
- One idea per message. Short lines. No marketing in a transactional text.
- Say it is automated, and how to reach a person (S72).
- Name SOS SMS before any link, and never send a link first (S59).
- A message asking for payment states the trip charge, what it covers, and the guarantee scope in the same thread.

## Writing for the ear (the ten-minute partner explainer)
- Short spoken sentences, second person, read aloud before it ships.
- Nothing pre-launch shown as working. Mockups carry "Pre-launch. Not live." on screen.

## Pre-launch framing
Contractor recruiting (cold email, partner materials, the contractor page) goes out before launch, by design. It describes what SOS SMS is building, in the future tense: "SOS SMS is building ...", "When it opens in {{zone}} ...". Never as a working service. Customer copy is written for launch day and held until the zone opens.

## Claim discipline
Every "only", "first", "best", "never", "always", and "guaranteed" is checked against `do-not-say.md` before it ships. Every capability is checked against `feature-status.md`: nothing is shipped, so nothing is claimed as live. A conditional claim names its condition in the same sentence: the zone, the covered hours, the scope of the guarantee. Neither side's copy may promise what the other side's terms do not deliver (`reference/marketplace-cross-side.md`).

## Channel discipline
- **Write for contractors:** cold email (four emails over twelve days, zone by zone), partner materials (counter card, newsletter blurb, trade school listing, partner kit, explainer script), the contractor page, the terms on one page, the Lead Cost Check, the Job Leak Scorecard, sign-up texts, the referral prompt.
- **Write for customers:** Google and Microsoft search ads, zone pages, problem guides, the guarantee page, the first reply and transactional texts, Text READY, the contact card and forwardable card, the "2 a.m. leak" lead magnet.
- **Refuse:** sales call scripts, demo scripts, meeting or shop-visit plans, LinkedIn ads or outreach, outbound email or texts to homeowners, marketing texts without written consent, Local Services Ads, partner pitches to agents, inspectors, hardware stores, or homeowner associations, neighborhood group posts, retargeting and pixels. Organic social and newsletters are out until a decision adds them. Say so, name the in-mix channel that does the job, and stop.

## Signature lines
Approved for internal use and for copy:
- "Every promise to a customer is kept by a contractor first."
- "One text out. One yes back." (brand cover line)
- "For fire, a gas smell, or anyone hurt, call 911 first." (the safety line)

Drafts that wait on a decision (never use before the decision is logged):
- "Lose the race, lose nothing." Contractors, waits on decision 4.
- "No more driving out for free." Contractors, waits on decision 3.
- "Ask once. Hear back from one." Customers, waits on conflict C.
- "The trip charge, before anyone is sent." Customers, never implies the repair price.

## Formatting
Prefer prose. Lists only for real lists: steps, terms, requirements. Headlines short and concrete, a mechanic rather than a mood.
