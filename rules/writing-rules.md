# Writing rules

These apply to every word of output, on both sides of the marketplace, no exceptions.
last_reviewed: 2026-10-11

## Mechanics
- No em dashes and no en dashes, anywhere, including ranges. Write "$25 to $60" and "9 p.m. to 6 a.m.".
- Banned words: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower. Their variants too (unlocked, empowering, streamlined).
- Always the full name, SOS SMS. Never the first word alone, in copy, file names, ad keywords, or alt text.
- Short paragraphs, one to three sentences.
- Mechanics over adjectives: who pays what, when, and what happens if it goes wrong.
- No stacked adjectives, no exclamation marks, no hype, and no superlative that is not cleared in `do-not-say.md`.
- Every value comes from `reference/resolution-register.md`: decided, a planning value labelled as one, or counted at a named stage. Per-message details are `{{merge fields}}`, filled from a named record and never typed by hand. Nothing is left as a gap or a placeholder.
- Numbers carry their source ID (S01 to S94). A grade C figure stays internal and shows its grade. Grade A and B figures go into external copy only after someone opens the URL and confirms the figure on the page: the research was read from search summaries.

## Tone
Dispatch-room calm: plain, exact, unhurried, each claim stated once. The subject is emergencies; the copy is not one. A homeowner deciding whether to join is calm, at a kitchen table, and wants the price, the scope, and how to cancel, stated once. A member who texts may be standing in water and needs the next step, not a pitch. The contractor has heard every platform pitch and trusts terms, not adjectives.

## Per side
- **Contractors.** Talk money and terms first: what they pay, when they are paid, what they commit to. Plain trade talk, no marketing gloss. The answer to "what's the catch" goes in the first screen.
- **Members, before the need.** The price, what membership includes, the trip charge on use, the guarantee with its scope, and how to cancel, all before the card. Ready, not afraid: no damage figures without a source, no fear of the flood.
- **Members, during the need.** The first reply says the request is going to the contractors now, the safety line, and how to reach a person; then the stop-the-water steps. No offer, no link, nothing to confirm. The safety line whenever copy invites an urgent text.

## Writing for a text message
Text is the product. Every member and contractor message follows these.
- One idea per message. Short lines. No marketing in a transactional text.
- Say it is automated, and how to reach a person (S72).
- Name SOS SMS before any link, and never send a link first (S59).
- A member's emergency texts never ask for a payment, a card, or a confirmation: the card is on file. The acceptance text states the trip charge, what it covers, and the guarantee scope in the same thread.

## Writing for the ear (the ten-minute partner explainer)
- Short spoken sentences, second person, read aloud before it ships.
- Nothing pre-launch shown as working. Mockups carry "Pre-launch. Not live." on screen.

## Pre-launch framing
Contractor recruiting (cold email, partner materials, the contractor page) goes out before launch, by design. It describes what SOS SMS is building, in the future tense: "SOS SMS is building ...", "When it opens in {{zone}} ...". Never as a working service. On the member side, the readiness scorecard, prevention guides, and the launch list go out from Stage 1 and promise no service; membership copy is written for launch day and held until memberships open in the zone (R55).

## Claim discipline
Every "only", "first", "best", "never", "always", and "guaranteed" is checked against `do-not-say.md` before it ships. Every capability is checked against `feature-status.md`: nothing is shipped, so nothing is claimed as live. A conditional claim names its condition in the same sentence: the zone, the covered hours, the scope of the guarantee. Neither side's copy may promise what the other side's terms do not deliver (`reference/marketplace-cross-side.md`).

## Channel discipline
- **Write for contractors:** cold email (four emails over twelve days, zone by zone), partner materials (counter card, newsletter blurb, trade school listing, partner kit, explainer script), the contractor page, the terms on one page, the Lead Cost Check, the Job Leak Scorecard, sign-up texts, the referral prompt.
- **Write for members:** affiliate kits and partner sends (with the disclosure), the launch list and member email (welcome, seasonal notes, renewal), the membership page with the ZIP check, zone pages, prevention and problem guides, the guarantee page, search and social ads for sign-ups in open zones, organic social and community posts by SOS SMS itself, the readiness scorecard ("How Ready Is Your Home for a 2 a.m. Leak?"), Text READY, the welcome texts, the first reply and transactional texts, the contact card and forwardable card.
- **Refuse:** sales call scripts, demo scripts, meeting, shop-visit, or door-to-door plans, LinkedIn ads or outreach, cold email or texts to homeowners, marketing texts without written consent, Local Services Ads, ads that sell dispatch to someone in the middle of an emergency, retargeting and pixels, and any payment to someone referring at a home purchase settlement (real estate agents, lenders, title and settlement agents; agents and inspectors may share SOS SMS without payment). Say so, name the in-mix channel that does the job, and stop.

## Signature lines

- "Every promise to a member is kept by a contractor first."
- "One text, and help is on the way." (the members' brand promise; only where memberships are open, with the safety line)
- "Join before you need it." (the members' sub tag)
- "One text out. One yes back." (brand cover line)
- "For fire, a gas smell, or anyone hurt, call 911 first." (the safety line)

## Formatting
Prefer prose. Lists only for real lists: steps, terms, requirements. Headlines short and concrete, a mechanic rather than a mood.
