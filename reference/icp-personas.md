# ICP and buying committee

Load for: who an asset is for, who blocks, what wins each level, and how to find them. Distills both runs' Phase 4 and Phase 5. Every row applies inside the founding zone ({TBD}).

## Contractors

| | Level 1: word-of-mouth emergency taker | Level 2: already paying for jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Owner-operator plumbers and water-emergency contractors living on referrals | Small plumbing and water damage shops, owner plus one to five technicians, with at least one paid lead source |
| Pain | Unpaid trips; night calls; slow weeks; no budget for lead platforms | Cost per booked job; shared leads; leads that never reply; after-hours dispatch overhead (S03 to S08) |
| Hypothesis | Overflow emergencies in hours they choose, costing nothing when quiet, with the trip paid before they roll | No per-lead cost; a request they do not win costs nothing; the trip charge collected before they roll |
| Triggers | Slow season; a run of unpaid trips; a new helper or truck | A lead platform renewal or price rise; a month of unreplied leads; a new technician; freeze season |
| Guarantee fit | Limited: one person cannot always roll at 2 a.m. | Good: an on-call technician can |

**Level 2 goes first:** it has a savings anchor (cost per booked job today), night capacity, and has already accepted platforms. **Level 1 is recruited alongside** to fill hours and refer other trades. **Disqualifiers:** unlicensed or uninsured; won't credit the trip charge; won't commit to an arrival time.

### Committee
- **Level 1:** the owner-operator is user, manager, and decision maker. Blocks on: burned by a platform, suspects a catch in "no fees", won't commit at 2 a.m., no more apps. Wins with: the terms on one page, how a job works in three texts, the Lead Cost Check.
- **Level 2:** technician (user), dispatcher (manager), owner (decision maker). **The dispatcher is the likely blocker**, because a request accepted on a technician's phone goes around the dispatch board. Settle who receives requests before the first one arrives. The owner blocks on platform distrust (S01), a race to the bottom, and liability for a missed arrival. Win the dispatcher with where an SOS SMS job enters their flow, a sample payout record, and the dispute FAQ.

### The founding cohort
Licensed, insured, takes emergency calls, spare capacity some nights, willing to credit the trip charge and commit to an arrival time, willing to give feedback. Sized by `density-model.md`. Found by cold email, supply house counters, the PHCC chapter (S24), and referrals.

## Customers

| | Level 1: no go-to contractor | Level 2: a contractor who does not answer at night |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Homeowner, often a recent buyer or new to the area | Established homeowner; sometimes a small landlord with one or two rentals |
| Pain | Who to trust; where the shutoff is; what a fair price is | Voicemail at 2 a.m.; unexpected after-hours fees; no-shows (S50, S51) |
| Hypothesis | One text reaches vetted contractors; an arrival time; the trip charge before anyone comes | The text to send when their contractor does not answer, with a money-backed window |
| Triggers | The emergency; moving in; a neighbor's flood story; the first freeze | The emergency after hours; their contractor retiring or booked up |

Both levels are reached at the same moment: when they search for help. Buy the first request through paid search inside zones at density; earn every later one through the saved contact card, the forwardable card, and Text READY.

### Committee
It collapses to the person at home, deciding in minutes. **The blocker is a payment link from an unknown number**: text scams cost consumers $470 million in 2024 (S59). Others: no card at hand (76.4 percent of households had a credit card, 4.2 percent were unbanked, S61), fear of being overcharged, wanting a voice. Wins with: the first reply that says who SOS SMS is, what happens next, and the trip charge before any link; then the contractor's name, rating, and arrival time. Level 2 also needs to hear they can keep their usual contractor for daytime work.

### Scope
The founding customer area is the neighborhoods inside the founding contractor coverage, nowhere else. Renters are out (the landlord is a second decision maker). Life-safety emergencies go to 911 and the utility first (S60). Property managers would be a third run.

## Permission rules (the only path to proof)
Contractors: a permission clause before the first job. Customers: a terms clause and a two-question follow-up text (arrived within the window? tell a neighbor?), recorded verbatim with the date. A quote enters `ops/copy-bank.md` only with permission on record.
