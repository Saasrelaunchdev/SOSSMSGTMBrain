# ICP and buying committee

Load for: who an asset is for, who blocks, what wins each level, and how to find them. Distills both runs' Phase 4 and Phase 5 (the member side revised by the membership revision, 2026-10-11; both sides by the tiers revision, 2026-10-20: plumbing, HVAC, and electrical). Every row applies inside the launch zone: a 10-mile radius around the first contractors' shops in the founders' home metro (decision 1).

## Contractors

| | Level 1: word-of-mouth emergency taker | Level 2: already paying for jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Owner-operator plumbers, HVAC technicians, and electricians who take emergency calls and live on referrals | Small plumbing, HVAC, and electrical shops, owner plus one to five technicians, with at least one paid lead source. Water damage and locksmith come later |
| Pain | Unpaid trips; night calls; slow weeks; no budget for lead platforms | Cost per booked job; shared leads; leads that never reply; after-hours dispatch overhead (S03 to S08) |
| Hypothesis | Overflow emergencies in hours they choose, costing nothing when quiet, with the trip paid before they roll | No per-lead cost; a request they do not win costs nothing; the trip charge collected before they roll |
| Triggers | Slow season; a run of unpaid trips; a new helper or truck | A lead platform renewal or price rise; a month of unreplied leads; a new technician; freeze season |
| Licenses | The state license for each trade taken, and insurance | The same, held by the shop; a shop licensed in more than one trade counts in each (R08) |
| Guarantee fit | Limited: one person cannot always roll at 2 a.m. | Good: an on-call technician can |

**Level 2 goes first:** it has a savings anchor (cost per booked job today), night capacity in each trade, and has already accepted platforms. **Level 1 is recruited alongside** to fill hours and refer other trades. **Disqualifiers:** unlicensed or uninsured; won't credit the trip charge; won't commit to an arrival time.

### Committee
- **Level 1:** the owner-operator is user, manager, and decision maker. Blocks on: burned by a platform, suspects a catch in "no fees", won't commit at 2 a.m., no more apps. Wins with: the terms on one page, how a job works in three texts, the Lead Cost Check.
- **Level 2:** technician (user), dispatcher (manager), owner (decision maker). **The dispatcher is the likely blocker**, because a request accepted on a technician's phone goes around the dispatch board. Settle who receives requests before the first one arrives. The owner blocks on platform distrust (S01), a race to the bottom, and liability for a missed arrival (a late arrival keeps the trip charge; a no-show is paid nothing, R67). Win the dispatcher with where an SOS SMS job enters their flow, a sample payout record, and the dispute FAQ.

### The first cohort
Licensed for each trade taken, insured, takes emergency calls, spare capacity some nights, willing to credit the trip charge and commit to an arrival time, willing to give feedback. 9 contractors per trade, 27 places across the three trades (R08, planning value), sized by `density-model.md`; on the same terms as every later contractor, joining any time. Found by cold email, supply house counters in each trade, the PHCC, ACCA, NECA, and IEC chapters (S24 for PHCC), and referrals.

## Members

| | Level 1: a new homeowner without a go-to contractor | Level 2: an established homeowner whose plumber, HVAC company, or electrician does not answer at night |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Homeowner, often a recent buyer or new to the area (first-time buyers 24 percent of buyers in the 2024 profile, S91); no plumber, HVAC company, or electrician they would call | Established homeowner, usually in an older home; sometimes a small landlord with one or two rentals, one membership per address (R54) |
| Pain | Who to trust; who would come at night; where the shutoff or the breaker panel is; what a fair call-out costs | Voicemail at 2 a.m.; unexpected after-hours fees; no-shows (S50, S51); a warranty claim that took days (S20) |
| Hypothesis | Ready before anything breaks: one number saved, the card and address on file, so a plumbing, HVAC, or electrical emergency is one text to vetted contractors, with an arrival time when one accepts, and on Plus the 90-minute guarantee | The text to send when their contractor does not answer: many vetted contractors at once, an arrival time from the text, the guarantee on Plus, and their own contractors kept for daytime work |
| Triggers | Moving in and setting up services; the first freeze or heat wave; a neighbor's flood story; a home inspection that flagged old plumbing, an old furnace, or an old panel | An emergency where nobody answered; a warranty claim denied or slow; their contractor retiring or booked up; freeze and heat-wave seasons |
| Found through | Utility new-account offers, association welcome packets, the readiness scorecard, prevention guides, agents and inspectors who share SOS SMS without payment | Email from their association or insurance agent (existing policyholders), prevention guides, organic search at freeze season, a neighbor's forwarded card |
| Plan fit | Any plan; Plus for the guarantee behind the arrival time and for everyone at home able to send the text (up to 5 phones) | Same; Premier for a member with a second home, or who wants a trip charge a year paid by SOS SMS (R66) |

Both levels join before the need, through the partners and lists that already reach them, never through an ad at the moment of the emergency. Grow each zone's launch list during Stage 1; email it first when memberships open; let affiliates and email carry sign-ups, with search and SEO pages and paid social in support; no paid search at launch. Cost per new paid member by channel against the ceiling of the plan joined (R63).

### Committee
A household: one or two adults, deciding over days, at a kitchen table or in an inbox. **The blocker is "another subscription"**: a fee every month or year before anything breaks (R46). Others: "it rarely happens"; "is my home covered at night"; "which plan"; "what does it cost when I use it"; "how is this different from my warranty"; a card on file with a company they do not know yet; "can I cancel". Wins with: the plans and the trip charge on use stated plainly, as separate lines; the guarantee with its plans and scope; cancel any time; the readiness scorecard; a trusted partner's mention; the privacy line. Level 2 also needs to hear they keep their usual contractors for daytime work, and that a warranty, a plumbing plan, or an HVAC club pays for repairs or maintenance while SOS SMS gets someone there.

### Scope
The launch member area is the neighborhoods inside the first cohort's coverage, nowhere else, and memberships open there only when each of the three trades meets the density number in every hour band (R15, R55). Renters are out (the landlord is a second decision maker). Life-safety emergencies (gas, smoke, fire, sparks, a burning smell, a shock, injury) go to 911 and the utility first, member or not (S60, decision 8). Property managers would be a third run. About 410 members per zone, at 0.50 requests a member a year, give each first-cohort plumber about one request a month, an HVAC contractor about 0.8, and an electrician about one every 5 months (planning values, R58, R64).

## Permission rules (the only path to proof)
Contractors: a permission clause before the first job. Members: a clause in the member terms and a two-question follow-up text (arrived within the window? tell a neighbor?), recorded verbatim with the date. A quote enters `ops/copy-bank.md` only with permission on record.
