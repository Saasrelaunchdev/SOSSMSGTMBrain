## Who each side weighs SOS SMS against

**Contractors:** C1 Angi Leads, C2 Thumbtack, C3 Google Local Services Ads, C4 Service Direct, C5 home warranty networks (American Home Shield, Frontdoor), C6 the status quo (word of mouth and their own listing). Answering services are a cost, not a source of jobs, so they appear only as a pricing anchor. Dropped: Jiffy and Puls (revisit when the metro is set); eLocal, Networx, Porch, Yelp, Bark (same model, less share of mind).

**Members**, what a household sets up or relies on before anything breaks: D1 nothing in advance (search and call when it happens), D2 plumber club memberships (Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Roto-Rooter), D3 home warranties (American Home Shield, Choice Home Warranty), D4 utility-partner plumbing plans (HomeServe), D5 home app memberships (Frontdoor), D6 on-demand marketplaces (Angi, Thumbtack). The pay per request slate (Roto-Rooter, Benjamin Franklin, Angi, Thumbtack, American Home Shield as emergency options) is kept as the record in the research.

## What the reviews say
- **Contractors praise** volume, reach, exclusivity (C4), and budget control (C3, C4). **They criticize** paying for leads that never reply, the same request sent to several pros (C1, C2), unsubstantiated quality claims (C1, S01), warranty rates and price control (C5), and answering the phone themselves at night (C6).
- **Homeowners praise** paying nothing until it breaks (D1), priority and discounts (D2), covered repairs (D3), plans that arrive through a trusted utility (D4), help in minutes by video (D5), and free requests with guarantees on booked work (D6). **They criticize** unanswered calls and surprise fees (D1), upselling and one company's schedule (D2), slow dispatch and fees on denied claims (D3), caps and a waiting period (D4), advice without a visit (D5), and being contacted by many (D6).

## The scenario each side creates
- **Contractors:** SOS SMS is the obvious choice for contractors in the launch zone who already pay per lead, take emergency calls, and want to stop paying for requests that never become paid trips. It is not the choice for someone who wants exclusive calls or maximum volume. Do not pretend otherwise.
- **Members:** the obvious choice for homeowners inside a dense founding zone who want the emergency itself to be one text, with a committed arrival time and money back if it is missed. Not for a household that wants its repairs paid for (warranties, utility plans) or one brand it already trusts at the door (a plumber's club). Many members will hold a warranty or a plan as well: those pay for the repair, SOS SMS gets someone there.

## The wedge
Every lead platform earns when a request arrives, so none can stop charging to look without changing its model, and none handles payment for the visit. Every plan a household can buy leaves the emergency itself alone: warranties and utility plans pay after a claim and a wait, and a plumber's club gives priority with one company, which cannot broadcast to rivals. A quote site earns from everyone calling back. Defensibility is local density, not technology: a rival needs the same contractors in a zone before it can make the same one-text, money-backed promise.

## Do not contest
Volume and exclusivity (contractors). Repair coverage, brand familiarity, and guarantees on the work (members). SOS SMS checks licenses and insurance, not workmanship, and pays for no repair, and says so.
