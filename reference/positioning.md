# Positioning statements

Load for: headlines, page openers, the one-line answer to "what is SOS SMS", and comparison copy. Distills both runs' Phase 3 (`phase-3-positioning-statements.md`) and Phase 2 positioning summaries (the member side revised by the membership revision, 2026-10-11; both sides by the tiers revision, 2026-10-20, and the pricing revision, 2026-10-21). Every statement carries the conditions that must be true before it leaves the company.

**Category:** an emergency home repair membership, by text; a local two-sided network. **Niche:** plumbing, HVAC, and electrical emergencies in one membership, one zone at a time.

## Contractors

**One line:** "For licensed plumbing, HVAC, and electrical contractors who are tired of paying for requests that never become work, SOS SMS is the emergency dispatch line where seeing a job costs nothing and the trip is paid before you roll."

| Against | Statement | Must be true before use |
|---|---|---|
| Pay-per-lead platforms | Not another lead service: a dispatch line for emergency jobs where you never pay to see a request. You accept the jobs you want, a request you do not win costs you nothing, and the trip charge is collected before you roll | No per-request fee in the revenue model (decision 2); the trip charge released on arrival, late or not, and the no-show rule written in the terms on one page (decision 3, R67) |
| Answering services, warranty networks, your own ads | A way to take emergency work at your own rates for the work without paying for the phone to ring. No monthly fee, nobody discounts your price for the work, you set the hours | No monthly fee; contractors set their price for the work. Never "your price" about the trip charge (decision 5) |
| The status quo | Not a replacement for your regulars: an overflow line for the emergencies you would take anyway, in the hours you choose. Quiet costs nothing; booked, you decline | No subscription; declining carries no penalty |

| Against | Statement | Must be true before use |
|---|---|---|

As a member, the emergency is one text, whether it is a burst pipe, no heat, or a dead circuit: it reaches the licensed, insured contractors on SOS SMS for that trade near your home at once, and the first who can come commits to an arrival time inside 90 minutes of your text. If they are late, we credit the membership before your request: 6 months on Plus, 12 on Premier | Memberships open in the zone: each trade at the density number in every hour band (R15, R55); the guarantee named with its plans and scope in the same place (decision 3). Every member statement that describes the emergency text carries the safety line.

## Differentiation and defensibility
Lead platforms cannot stop charging per request without changing their model, and none holds the visit payment before the contractor rolls. On the member side, a rival needs the same contractor density in a zone, in all three trades, before it can make the same one-text promise backed by a guarantee, so density is both the constraint and the moat. A number saved in a phone before the pipe bursts is worth more than an ad seen during the flood. Growth is bounded by supply: each metro and trade needs its own first cohort.

## The weak point to say first
- **Contractors:** every request is shared. "A request you do not win costs you nothing."
- **Members:** a fee every month or year, before anything breaks. Say it first, then what the plans include, the trip charge only on use, and cancel any time.

## Deliberately not claimed
More jobs or any volume; exclusive requests; better leads than a named rival; "zero cost" without the revenue model; a "guaranteed" trip charge without the late-arrival rule; "every contractor in your area"; "covered everywhere" or "24/7" (write "any hour, in `{{zone neighborhoods}}`"); any arrival average; the guarantee on Basic, or without its plans; "priority" or a faster plan; that SOS SMS prevents damage or saves money; that any plan pays for repairs or discounts them; a guarantee on the repair; "emergency service"; "never shared".
