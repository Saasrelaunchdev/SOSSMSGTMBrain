# Value proposition: customers

Load for: any customer-facing headline, ad, page, first reply, or transactional text. Distills Run 2 Phase 6 (`phase-6-value-proposition.md`). Every social-proof headline is a method line and every proof point a named record (R44, R45): no result exists before launch. No drill states or implies an average arrival time, a count of homes helped, or that SOS SMS prevents damage. Every value below is decided; per-message details are `{{merge fields}}`.

**Safety line, on every drill that invites a text:** "For fire, a gas smell, or anyone hurt, call 911 first." Gas variant (S60): "Smell gas? Get out first, then call 911 and your gas company."

**Brand promise:** Text once, and a vetted contractor is on the way, with the trip charge stated before anyone comes.
**Positioning:** The emergency repair line you text instead of calling down a list. Built on one message that reaches the vetted contractors for the trade at once, and an arrival window that costs SOS SMS money when it is missed.

## Pillars

| Pillar | Reasons to believe |
|---|---|
| VP1 On the way, inside a guaranteed window | One text reaches the licensed, insured contractors on SOS SMS for that trade nearby, all at once. An arrival time when they accept. If they are not there within 90 minutes of your request, the $99 trip charge comes back, and SOS SMS keeps working to get someone there |
| VP2 One text, one reply | Works entirely by text: no app, no phone tree. Only the contractor who takes your job gets your address and details. No selling your information, no ad targeting |
| VP3 Know the cost and the contractor first | The trip charge before anyone is sent, credited if you go ahead. License and insurance checked before they can take a job. The contractor's Google rating, where they have one |

## Method substitutes (use in place of social proof)
- "Your text goes to every matched contractor at once. The first who can come accepts." (drill 1)
- "When a contractor accepts, you get their name and arrival time by text." (drill 2)
- "The guarantee, in one line, before you pay anything: 90 minutes from your request, or the trip charge back." (drill 3)
- "Save one number. Text it. That is the whole setup." (drill 4)
- "Contractors see the problem and the neighborhood. The one who accepts gets your address." (drill 5)
- "Step one, we text you the trip charge. Step two, you agree. Step three, we send someone." (drill 7)
- "What we check: license, insurance, and when we check again." (drill 8)

## Body lines (25 words max)
- Drill 1: "Text what broke. It reaches the vetted contractors on SOS SMS for that trade nearby, all at once. The first who can come accepts."
- Drill 3: "If your contractor isn't there within 90 minutes of your request, the $99 trip charge comes back, and we keep working. Covered: {{zone neighborhoods}}, {{covered hours}}."
- Drill 7: "Before we send anyone, you see the $99 trip charge, credited to the repair if you go ahead, and the $49 SOS SMS fee."

## Cross-side holds
Drill 3 runs on the decided pair: the fault-based late-arrival rule in the contractor terms and the guarantee scope (decision 3). Drill 2 needs the contractor's arrival commitment in the terms on one page. Drill 7 needs crediting in the contractor agreement. Drill 8 uses the same recheck cadence on both sides (decision 7). "Licensed and insured" is never applied to SOS SMS itself.

## Proof capture
Permission clause in the terms of service before the first customer. After every job, one text: "Did your contractor arrive within the window? Would you tell a neighbor?" Verbatim, dated. Arrival times logged from acceptance to arrival, counted weekly from the first request (R43); a real figure is cited only with its sample size, its date, and permission.
